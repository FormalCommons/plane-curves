# Design of part T5: Noether's formula for intersection numbers at infinitely near points

Part T5 of [`DESIGN.md`](../DESIGN.md#stage-3-555-56-and-72-74) proves the
global form of the milestone **7-noether-formula**, *Noether's formula for
intersection numbers at infinitely near points*, of
[`atlas/03-resolution-of-singularities.md`](https://github.com/FormalCommons/plane-curves-atlas/blob/main/atlas/03-resolution-of-singularities.md#74-quadratic-transformations)
(Fulton §7.4, Problems 7.15 and 7.16, pp. 91–92). B4 has proved the local form
(`Affine.intersectionNumber_eq_mul_add_finsum_blowupStrictTransform`). This
file gives T5's files, its definitions and their API, its main results and
their names, what it uses, and the pieces it is built in. The pieces are listed
in [Pieces](#pieces), in order. Each is meant to become one issue and one pull
request of a few hundred lines.

The library declarations named here were found in the repository at
`21e8998` and in the pinned sources. Nothing was compiled. Names of new
declarations are proposals, as in `DESIGN.md`.

## Notation

Everything is in the namespace `PlaneCurves.Projective`, over a field `k`,
with `[IsAlgClosed k]` where needed. The notation of
[`design/T3.md`](T3.md#notation) and [`design/T4.md`](T4.md#notation) is used
throughout. These names are already in the library:

- `fundamentalPoint i`, `fundamentalTriangle`, `quadraticMap`, `properTransform`;
- `IsExcellentPosition`, `IsQuadraticTransformAt`, `IsQuadraticTransform`;
- `pointsOverCentre`, `virtualGenus` (`g*`), `nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints`.

`P₀ = fundamentalPoint 2 = [0 : 0 : 1]` is the centre in standard position.
`F` and `G` are forms of degrees `m` and `n`. `m_P(F) = mult F P` takes values
in `ℕ∞`. "`F` and `G` have no common tangent at `P`" is written inline, as
`intersectionNumber_eq_mul_mult_iff` and `finsum_mult_mul_mult_le_spec` already
write it: `¬∃ L, IsTangentAt F P L ∧ IsTangentAt G P L`. The atlas's book form
uses indices `1, …, s`. The library counts from `0`: `F₀ = F`, and the
`i`-th step goes from `Fᵢ` to `Fᵢ₊₁`.

## The mathematics, and the route

### 1. The statement

The book applies the same quadratic transformations to `F` and `G` by applying
them to `FG`. A step is centred at a point `P`. A change of coordinates `T`,
given by an invertible matrix `M`, puts `(FG)^T` in excellent position with
`T(P₀) = P`, and the step then takes proper transforms. The existing
`IsQuadraticTransformAt F P F'` hides `M` behind an `∃`. That loses two things
that T5 needs: the same `M` for `F` and for `G`, and the neighbouring
singularities, which are the points over the centre *in the coordinates of that
step*. So T5 makes the matrices explicit. A sequence is a function
`M : ℕ → Matrix (Fin 3) (Fin 3) k` together with a length `s`, and the curves
along it are computed:

```
quadraticTransformSeq M F 0       = F
quadraticTransformSeq M F (i + 1) = properTransform (linearSubst (M i) (quadraticTransformSeq M F i))
```

`IsQuadraticTransformSeq M F s` says that each of the first `s` steps is a
quadratic transformation: `M i` is invertible and
`linearSubst (M i) (quadraticTransformSeq M F i)` is in excellent position.
`properTransform` and `linearSubst` are multiplicative, so
`quadraticTransformSeq M (F * G) i = quadraticTransformSeq M F i * quadraticTransformSeq M G i`.
The pair `(Fᵢ, Gᵢ)` of the book is therefore `quadraticTransformSeq M F i` and
`quadraticTransformSeq M G i`, under `IsQuadraticTransformSeq M (F * G) s`.
This matches T4: `IsQuadraticTransformSeq.isChain` turns a sequence into T4's
`List.IsChain IsQuadraticTransform`.

**The neighbouring singularities** `P_{i+1, j}` are the points of `Fᵢ₊₁Gᵢ₊₁`
over the centre of the `i`-th step:
`pointsOverCentre (linearSubst (M i) (quadraticTransformSeq M (F * G) i))`.
By T3 these are the points of the new curve on `V(Z)` that are not fundamental,
the `[α : 1 : 0]` that correspond to the tangents at the centre
(`IsExcellentPosition.mem_pointsOverCentre_iff`). No new definition is needed.

**The index sets.** The first sum `Σ_P m_P(F) m_P(G)` runs over all of `ℙ²`
for the *first* curves `F` and `G`, as a `finsum`. Only common points
contribute, and there are finitely many of them. The book's second sum, over
`i > 1` in its numbering, is `∑ i ∈ Finset.range s` here, over the points over
the centre of each step. The book's formula is right as stated: the
derivation below proves it exactly. The fundamental points of the later curves
do not appear in it, because their contributions cancel against the growth of
the degrees. So `ERRATA.md` needs no entry.

**The hypotheses on the last pair.** The book asks that `F_s` and `G_s` have
only ordinary multiple points and no common tangents at common points. The
formula needs only the second condition: at a common point with no common
tangent, `I = m·m` (`intersectionNumber_eq_mul_mult_iff`). So T5 states the
formula for every sequence whose last pair has no common tangent, which is
more general. The ordinary multiple points enter only in the existence of a
sequence (§4). The inequality holds for *every* sequence of an irreducible
form (§5), whether or not it ends at ordinary multiple points. Both
generalizations keep the book's conclusion under weaker hypotheses, so they
need no `ERRATA.md` entry.

### 2. The route

**Route (a), chosen.** Put `D(F, G) := mn − Σ_P m_P(F) m_P(G)`. The key fact is
a one-step identity. Let `FG` be in excellent position, let
`r = m_{P₀}(F)`, `t = m_{P₀}(G)`, and let `F'`, `G'` be the proper transforms.
Then:

- `F'` has degree `2m − r` (`IsExcellentPosition.isHomogeneous_properTransform`).
- `F'` has multiplicity `m` at `P₀` and `m − r` at the other two fundamental
  points (`IsExcellentPosition.mult_properTransform_fundamentalPoint_two`,
  `IsExcellentPosition.mult_properTransform_fundamentalPoint`), and the same
  holds for `G'`.
- Off the triangle, `quadraticMap` is an involution that preserves
  multiplicities (`bijOn_quadraticMap_compl_fundamentalTriangle`,
  `quadraticMap_quadraticMap`,
  `mult_properTransform_of_notMem_fundamentalTriangle`).
- On the triangle, the only common point of `F` and `G` is `P₀`: excellent
  position of `FG` gives `I(Q, FG ∩ Xⱼ) = 1` at every other point `Q`, so `Q`
  lies on only one of the two curves.
- The points of `F'` on the triangle are fundamental or over the centre
  (`IsExcellentPosition.eq_fundamentalPoint_or_mem_pointsOverCentre`).

Write `O` for the part of the sum off the triangle, which is the same before
and after the step, and `J = Σ_{Q over the centre} m_Q(F') m_Q(G')`. Then

```
Σ_P m_P(F) m_P(G)    = r t + O
Σ_Q m_Q(F') m_Q(G')  = m n + 2 (m − r)(n − t) + J + O
(2m − r)(2n − t)     = 4mn − 2mt − 2nr + rt
```

so `D(F, G) = D(F', G') + J`. Without subtraction this reads

```
(2m − r)(2n − t) + Σ_P m_P(F) m_P(G) + J = m n + Σ_Q m_Q(F') m_Q(G').
```

This is pure arithmetic over T3's results. With `mult_linearSubst` and a
reindexing of the `finsum` by the bijection `T`, as in `virtualGenus_linearSubst`,
it holds for every step of a sequence. Summing along the sequence gives
`D(F, G) = D(F_s, G_s) + Σᵢ Jᵢ`. At the end, Bézout
(`finsum_intersectionNumber_eq_mul`, Q12) and `I = m·m` at every point give
`D(F_s, G_s) = 0`. This proves the formula. Bézout is used once, at the last
pair, and intersection numbers appear nowhere else.

**Route (b), not chosen.** Start from Bézout for `F, G` and expand each
`I(P, F ∩ G)` at a centre by B4's local form. That needs three things:

- the identification of a neighbourhood of `P₀` with B4's chart (T3 has it:
  `localRingEquivPointsOverCentre`, `intersectionNumber_properTransform_X_two_mk`);
- a transfer `I(P, F ∩ G) = I(Q(P), F' ∩ G')` off the triangle, through
  `localRingEquivQuadraticMap` (new, about 150 lines);
- the same bookkeeping of the points on the triangle and the fundamental points
  as (a), with `I` in place of `m·m`.

It still needs a last pair with no common tangent. So (b) is (a) plus about
400 lines. (a) is about 1100 lines over all pieces, and (b) about 1500.
B4 stays the local form of the milestone, and nothing in T5 uses it.

### 3. Transfer of intersection numbers

Route (a) needs no transfer of intersection numbers and no identification of
the points over the centre with B4's chart points `(0, α)`. T3 already proves
both of the facts the route uses about the transformation: multiplicities off
the triangle are preserved (through the local-ring isomorphism
`localRingEquivQuadraticMap`), and the points over the centre are
`[α : 1 : 0]` with `F_r(1, α) = 0`. A transfer of `I` along
`localRingEquivQuadraticMap` would be a natural lemma for later work (δ
invariants). T5 does not add it, because nothing in T5 uses it.

### 4. Common tangents, and the existence of a sequence

The notion is located, not defined: `¬∃ L, IsTangentAt F P L ∧ IsTangentAt G P L`.
For relatively prime forms, `D(F, G) = Σ_P (I(P, F ∩ G) − m_P(F) m_P(G))` by
Bézout. So `D ≥ 0` (`finsum_mult_mul_mult_le`, Q13), and `D = 0` exactly when
`F` and `G` have no common tangent at any point
(`mul_mult_le_intersectionNumber`, `intersectionNumber_eq_mul_mult_iff`).

**Quadratic transformations separate the tangents.** Suppose the step is centred
at a common point with a common tangent `L`. In standard position,
`L = aX + bY` with `a, b ≠ 0`, because good position says that `V(X)` and
`V(Y)` are not tangent at `P₀`. `L` divides both tangent cones, so
`[−b⁻¹a : 1 : 0]`, with the order of coordinates as in
`IsExcellentPosition.mem_pointsOverCentre_iff`, is over the centre for both `F`
and `G`. So `J ≥ 1`, and `D` drops by at least one. Since `D ≥ 0`, repeating
steps centred at common points with a common tangent ends in a pair with none.
This is the atlas's termination argument, with `D` in place of `I`.

**Excellent position for `FG`.** T3's `exists_isExcellentPosition_avoiding` is
for an irreducible form. For a squarefree `H = ∏ q_k`, with `q_k` pairwise
non-associated irreducible forms, `H` is in excellent position exactly when
each `q_k` is, and no two factors have a common point on the triangle other
than `P₀` (`isExcellentPosition_mul_iff`, and its product form). The existence
proof is T3's with more exclusions, all finite:

- The lines through `P` are chosen in the intersection of the cofinite sets of
  `eventually_cofinite_ncard_zeroLocus_pair_diff_eq_of_not_isTerriblePoint`
  for every factor (`Filter.eventually_all_finite`). They also avoid the lines
  joining `P` to the finitely many common points of two factors
  (`finite_zeroLocus_pair_of_isRelPrime`,
  `finite_setOf_eval_sum_C_mul_X_eq_zero_of_finite`).
- The third line is chosen through a point `R` off `H` that is not terrible for
  any factor. Each factor has at most one terrible point
  (`IsTerriblePoint.mk_eq_mk`), and `H` misses infinitely many points. The line
  is then chosen among the cofinitely many good lines through `R` for every
  factor, off `S` and the pairwise common points. This is the family version of
  `exists_ncard_zeroLocus_pair_sum_C_mul_X_eq`.

This needs `P` not to be terrible for any factor of `H`. In characteristic 0
that always holds (`not_isTerriblePoint`).

**Ordinary multiple points as well.** To reach the book's last pair, with
only ordinary multiple points and no common tangents, use the measure

```
μ(H) = Σ_k ( N(q_k) + g*(q_k) ) + Σ_{k<l} D(q_k, q_l)
```

over the irreducible factors of `H = FG`. Each term is a natural number
(`virtualGenus_nonneg`, `finsum_mult_mul_mult_le`). A step transforms each
factor into an irreducible factor of `H'`
(`IsQuadraticTransformAt.irreducible`, `properTransform_prod`). A point `P` is
a non-ordinary multiple point of `H` exactly when it is a non-ordinary multiple
point of some `q_k`, or two factors have a common tangent there, because the
tangent cone of the product is the product of the tangent cones. In the first
case T4's `IsQuadraticTransformAt.ncard_add_virtualGenus_lt` lowers
`N(q_k) + g*(q_k)`. In the second case the one-step identity lowers
`D(q_k, q_l)`. No other term grows: T4 gives
`IsQuadraticTransformAt.ncard_nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints_eq` and
`IsQuadraticTransformAt.virtualGenus_eq` at simple or ordinary centres, and
`D` never grows. So steps centred at non-ordinary multiple points of `H`
terminate, and they end with `H_s` having only ordinary multiple points.
This implies that `F_s` and `G_s` have only ordinary multiple points and no
common tangent at a common point. This existence is the book's Problem
7.16 (a) for a pair. T5 proves it in characteristic 0; see
[Notes](#notes) for characteristic `p`.

### 5. The inequality

T5 takes the `g*` route. T3's `virtualGenus_properTransform` and
`virtualGenus_linearSubst` give, along any sequence of an irreducible `F`,

```
2 g*(F) = 2 g*(F_s) + Σ_{i < s} Σ_{Q over the i-th centre} m_Q(Fᵢ₊₁)(m_Q(Fᵢ₊₁) − 1),
```

since `2·C(m, 2) = m(m − 1)`. `two_mul_virtualGenus` rewrites `2 g*(F)` as
`(n − 1)(n − 2) − Σ_P m_P(F)(m_P(F) − 1)`, and `virtualGenus_nonneg` for `F_s`
gives the inequality. This uses no second curve. The polar route, Noether's
formula for `F` and a polar curve, would need the formula for a pair that is
not squarefree and is longer. The equality is kept as a result of its own
(`IsQuadraticTransformSeq.two_mul_virtualGenus_eq`).

## Definitions and their API

### Sequences of quadratic transformations (`Sequence.lean`, T5.2)

- `quadraticTransformSeq (M : ℕ → Matrix (Fin 3) (Fin 3) k) (F : MvPolynomial (Fin 3) k) : ℕ → MvPolynomial (Fin 3) k`,
  by `Nat.rec` as in [§1](#1-the-statement): the curves `F = F₀, F₁, …` obtained
  by successive changes of coordinates `M i` and proper transforms. API:
  - `quadraticTransformSeq_zero`, `quadraticTransformSeq_succ` (`@[simp]`);
  - `quadraticTransformSeq_succ'`, the first step peeled off:
    `quadraticTransformSeq M F (i + 1) = quadraticTransformSeq (fun j ↦ M (j + 1)) (properTransform (linearSubst (M 0) F)) i`.
    The inductions along a sequence use this.
  - `quadraticTransformSeq_mul` (`F`, `G` homogeneous):
    `quadraticTransformSeq M (F * G) i = quadraticTransformSeq M F i * quadraticTransformSeq M G i`,
    from `properTransform_mul`, `map_mul` and `IsHomogeneous.properTransform`;
  - `isHomogeneous_quadraticTransformSeq`:
    `∃ d, (quadraticTransformSeq M F i).IsHomogeneous d` for homogeneous `F`.
- `IsQuadraticTransformSeq (M : ℕ → Matrix (Fin 3) (Fin 3) k) (F : MvPolynomial (Fin 3) k) (s : ℕ) : Prop :=
  ∀ i < s, Function.Injective (M i).mulVecLin ∧ IsExcellentPosition (linearSubst (M i) (quadraticTransformSeq M F i))`:
  "the first `s` steps are quadratic transformations". The `i`-th centre is
  `map (M i).mulVecLin _ (fundamentalPoint 2)`. API:
  - `isQuadraticTransformSeq_def`, `isQuadraticTransformSeq_zero` (`s = 0`
    always holds), and `isQuadraticTransformSeq_succ_iff`: the first step and
    the rest, through `quadraticTransformSeq_succ'`. The existence proofs
    build sequences with it, prepending a matrix by
    `fun i ↦ Nat.casesOn i M₀ M'`.
  - `IsQuadraticTransformSeq.mono` (`s' ≤ s`).
  - `IsQuadraticTransformSeq.of_mul_left`, `.of_mul_right` (homogeneous `F`,
    `G`): a sequence of `F * G` is one of `F` and of `G`, by
    `quadraticTransformSeq_mul` and `IsExcellentPosition.of_mul_left` (T5.1).
  - `IsQuadraticTransformSeq.isQuadraticTransformAt` (`i < s`):
    `IsQuadraticTransformAt (quadraticTransformSeq M F i) (map (M i).mulVecLin hM (fundamentalPoint 2)) (quadraticTransformSeq M F (i + 1))`,
    for the `hM` given by the hypothesis.
  - `IsQuadraticTransformSeq.isChain`:
    `List.IsChain IsQuadraticTransform ((List.range (s + 1)).map (quadraticTransformSeq M F))`.
    This links the sequence to T4's chains.
  - `IsQuadraticTransformSeq.irreducible` (irreducible `F`, `i ≤ s`), from
    `IsQuadraticTransformAt.irreducible`.
  - `IsQuadraticTransformSeq.isRelPrime` (`F`, `G` relatively prime, a sequence
    of `F * G`, `i ≤ s`): `IsRelPrime (quadraticTransformSeq M F i) (quadraticTransformSeq M G i)`,
    from `IsRelPrime.properTransform` (T5.1) and the invariance of `IsRelPrime`
    under the ring automorphism `linearSubst (M i)`.
  - Example: a nodal cubic, with one step centred at the node.

No other definition is needed. `D(F, G)` is written out
(`(m * n : ℕ∞) = Σᶠ P, mult F P * mult G P + …`), and so is the measure of
[§4](#4-common-tangents-and-the-existence-of-a-sequence), in `ℕ` through
`ENat.toNat` and `Int.toNat`, as T4 writes `μ`.

## Main results

### Products (T5.1)

- `isTangentAt_mul_iff` (`Tangent.lean`; `F`, `G` nonzero forms):
  `IsTangentAt (F * G) P L ↔ IsTangentAt F P L ∨ IsTangentAt G P L`.
  The proof uses `MvPolynomial.leadingFormAt_mul`, and that a linear form is
  prime.
- `isGoodPosition_mul_iff` (`GoodPosition.lean`):
  `IsGoodPosition (F * G) ↔ IsGoodPosition F ∧ IsGoodPosition G`.
- `isExcellentPosition_mul_iff` (`Position.lean`; `F`, `G` homogeneous):
  `IsExcellentPosition (F * G) ↔ IsExcellentPosition F ∧ IsExcellentPosition G ∧ ∀ j, ∀ Q ∈ zeroLocus {F, G, X j}, Q = fundamentalPoint 2`.
  The proof uses `intersectionNumber_mul_left`, the fact that `I(Q, F ∩ Xⱼ) ≥ 1`
  at a point of `F`, and `isGoodPosition_mul_iff`. Its corollaries are
  `IsExcellentPosition.of_mul_left`, `.of_mul_right`, and
  `IsExcellentPosition.eq_fundamentalPoint_two_of_mem`, which says that a
  common point on the triangle is `P₀`.
- `pointsOverCentre_mul` (`Singularities/Basic.lean`; homogeneous, nonzero):
  `pointsOverCentre (F * G) = pointsOverCentre F ∪ pointsOverCentre G`.
- `IsRelPrime.properTransform` (`Basic.lean`; homogeneous, no `X i ∣ F` or
  `X i ∣ G`): relatively prime forms have relatively prime proper transforms.
  `Squarefree.properTransform` says the same for squarefree. Proof: an
  irreducible common factor `h` of `F'` and `G'` is not an `X i`
  (`not_X_dvd_properTransform`). So `h'` is irreducible
  (`irreducible_properTransform`) and divides `F'' = F` and `G'' = G`
  (`properTransform_mul`, `properTransform_properTransform`).

### Two curves under one step (`Singularities/CommonPoints.lean`, T5.3)

For `h : IsExcellentPosition (F * G)`, `F` of degree `m`, `G` of degree `n`,
`r = (mult F P₀).toNat`, `t = (mult G P₀).toNat`:

- `IsExcellentPosition.mul_add_finsum_mult_mul_mult_properTransform`:
  `↑((2 * m - r) * (2 * n - t)) + ∑ᶠ P, mult F P * mult G P + ∑ᶠ Q ∈ pointsOverCentre (F * G), mult F' Q * mult G' Q = ↑(m * n) + ∑ᶠ Q, mult F' Q * mult G' Q`,
  in `ℕ∞`, with `F' = properTransform F`. The proof is the computation of
  [§2](#2-the-route). It splits each `finsum` into the triangle and its
  complement (`finsum_mem_union`-style lemmas). It assumes `IsRelPrime F G`,
  so that the supports are finite (`finite_zeroLocus_pair_of_isRelPrime`), and
  it reindexes the complement by `quadraticMap`.
- The same identity after a change of coordinates,
  `mul_add_finsum_mult_mul_mult_properTransform_linearSubst`, for `M` with
  `hM` and `IsExcellentPosition (linearSubst M (F * G))`, with the left sums
  of `F`, `G` (`mult_linearSubst`, and `finsum` along the bijection
  `map M.mulVecLin hM`).
- `IsExcellentPosition.exists_mem_pointsOverCentre_of_isTangentAt`: if
  `IsTangentAt F P₀ L` and `IsTangentAt G P₀ L`, there is
  `Q ∈ pointsOverCentre F ∩ pointsOverCentre G`. The proof is the argument of
  [§4](#4-common-tangents-and-the-existence-of-a-sequence), through
  `isTangentAt_iff_affine` and `mem_pointsOverCentre_iff`. Its corollary
  `IsExcellentPosition.one_le_finsum_mult_mul_mult_pointsOverCentre` gives
  `1 ≤ ∑ᶠ Q ∈ pointsOverCentre (F * G), mult F' Q * mult G' Q`.

### Along a sequence (`NeighbouringSingularities.lean`, T5.4)

Write `Fᵢ = quadraticTransformSeq M F i`, `Gᵢ` likewise, and
`Nᵢ = pointsOverCentre (linearSubst (M i) (quadraticTransformSeq M (F * G) i))`.

- `IsQuadraticTransformSeq.mul_add_finsum_mult_mul_mult` (no tangency
  hypothesis): for `h : IsQuadraticTransformSeq M (F * G) s`,
  `↑(m * n) + ∑ᶠ P, mult F_s P * mult G_s P = ↑(m_s * n_s) + ∑ᶠ P, mult F P * mult G P + ∑ i ∈ Finset.range s, ∑ᶠ Q ∈ Nᵢ, mult Fᵢ₊₁ Q * mult Gᵢ₊₁ Q`,
  where `m_s` and `n_s` are the degrees of `F_s` and `G_s`. This is the
  telescoped identity, by induction on `s` with `quadraticTransformSeq_succ'`.
- **Noether's formula**, `IsQuadraticTransformSeq.natCast_mul_eq_finsum_mult_mul_mult_add`
  (`[IsAlgClosed k]`, `IsRelPrime F G`, `F`, `G` nonzero, and
  `hT : ∀ P L, ¬ (IsTangentAt F_s P L ∧ IsTangentAt G_s P L)`):
  `↑(m * n) = ∑ᶠ P, mult F P * mult G P + ∑ i ∈ Finset.range s, ∑ᶠ Q ∈ Nᵢ, mult Fᵢ₊₁ Q * mult Gᵢ₊₁ Q`.
  From the previous result and Bézout for the last pair:
  `↑(m_s * n_s) = ∑ᶠ P, intersectionNumber P F_s G_s = ∑ᶠ P, mult F_s P * mult G_s P`,
  by `finsum_intersectionNumber_eq_mul`, `IsQuadraticTransformSeq.isRelPrime`
  and `intersectionNumber_eq_mul_mult_iff`. Cancellation in `ℕ∞` is fine,
  since the cancelled term is finite.
- `IsQuadraticTransformSeq.two_mul_virtualGenus_eq` (irreducible `F` and
  `h : IsQuadraticTransformSeq M F s`):
  `2 * virtualGenus F = 2 * virtualGenus F_s + ∑ i ∈ Finset.range s, ∑ᶠ Q ∈ pointsOverCentre (linearSubst (M i) Fᵢ), ((mult Fᵢ₊₁ Q).toNat : ℤ) * ((mult Fᵢ₊₁ Q).toNat - 1)`,
  from `virtualGenus_properTransform`, `virtualGenus_linearSubst` and
  `two_mul_choose_two`.
- **The inequality**,
  `IsQuadraticTransformSeq.finsum_mult_mul_mult_sub_one_add_le` (the same
  hypotheses, `F` of degree `n`):
  `∑ᶠ P, ((mult F P).toNat : ℤ) * ((mult F P).toNat - 1) + ∑ i ∈ Finset.range s, ∑ᶠ Q ∈ …, … ≤ ((n : ℤ) - 1) * ((n : ℤ) - 2)`,
  from the previous result, `two_mul_virtualGenus` and `virtualGenus_nonneg`.

### Excellent position for squarefree forms (`TerriblePoint.lean`, `Position.lean`; T5.5)

- `eventually_cofinite_forall_ncard_zeroLocus_pair_diff_eq_of_not_isTerriblePoint`
  (`TerriblePoint.lean`): for a finite family of irreducible forms none of
  which has `P` as a terrible point, the lines through `P` that are good for
  every form, as in the single-form lemma, are cofinite. Proof:
  `Filter.eventually_all_finite`.
- `exists_forall_ncard_zeroLocus_pair_sum_C_mul_X_eq` (`TerriblePoint.lean`,
  `[PerfectField k]`): the family version of
  `exists_ncard_zeroLocus_pair_sum_C_mul_X_eq`. It gives a line off a given
  finite set that meets each form transversally in `deg q_k` points.
- `isExcellentPosition_prod_iff` (`Position.lean`): for a `Finset` or
  `Multiset` of homogeneous forms, the product is in excellent position exactly
  when each form is, and no two of them have a common point on the triangle
  other than `P₀`. The proof is induction on `isExcellentPosition_mul_iff`.
- `exists_isExcellentPosition_avoiding_of_squarefree` (`Position.lean`):
  `exists_isExcellentPosition_avoiding` for a squarefree form `H` and a point
  `P` that is not terrible for any irreducible factor of `H`
  (`∀ q, Irreducible q → q ∣ H → ¬ IsTerriblePoint q P.rep`). The proof
  applies the private `isExcellentPosition_linearSubst_of_lines` to each factor
  (`UniqueFactorizationMonoid.factors`, with `prod_factors` up to a unit and
  `isExcellentPosition_smul_iff`), with lines from the two family lemmas, and
  then uses `isExcellentPosition_prod_iff`. Its corollary
  `exists_isExcellentPosition_of_squarefree_of_charZero` takes any `P`, by
  `not_isTerriblePoint`.

### Existence of sequences, and the milestone (`NeighbouringSingularities.lean`, T5.6)

- `exists_isQuadraticTransformSeq_forall_isOrdinaryMultiplePoint`
  (`[IsAlgClosed k] [CharZero k]`, `F`, `G` homogeneous,
  `Squarefree (F * G)`): there are `M` and `s` with
  `IsQuadraticTransformSeq M (F * G) s` and
  `∀ P, IsMultiplePoint (F_s * G_s) P → IsOrdinaryMultiplePoint (F_s * G_s) P`.
  The proof is strong induction on the measure `μ` of
  [§4](#4-common-tangents-and-the-existence-of-a-sequence), stated for a list
  of pairwise relatively prime irreducible forms, each step built with
  `exists_isExcellentPosition_of_squarefree_of_charZero` and
  `isQuadraticTransformSeq_succ_iff`. It needs these helper lemmas, which the
  piece places by topic:
  - a non-ordinary multiple point of a product is a non-ordinary multiple point
    of a factor, or a common tangent of two factors (`Tangent.lean`, from
    `MvPolynomial.leadingFormAt_mul` and the factorization of the leading form
    into tangents, `Affine.multiplicity_tangent_mul`);
  - the converse used at the end: at an ordinary multiple point of `F * G`, `F`
    and `G` have no common tangent (`IsOrdinaryMultiplePoint.not_isTangentAt_and`,
    since `L²` would divide the leading form);
  - `IsQuadraticTransformAt`-free one-step forms of T4's
    `ncard_add_virtualGenus_lt` and `virtualGenus_eq` for a factor, through
    `IsExcellentPosition.of_mul_left`.
- Its corollary `exists_isQuadraticTransformSeq_forall_not_isTangentAt`: the
  same `M` and `s`, with `F_s`, `G_s` having only ordinary multiple points and
  no common tangent at any point. This is the book's last pair.
- **The milestone**, `natCast_mul_eq_finsum_mult_mul_mult_add_spec`, in the
  `…_spec` form of `finsum_mult_mul_mult_le_spec`. It combines:
  1. Noether's formula, `IsQuadraticTransformSeq.natCast_mul_eq_finsum_mult_mul_mult_add`,
     for every sequence of `F * G` whose last pair has no common tangent;
  2. the inequality, `IsQuadraticTransformSeq.finsum_mult_mul_mult_sub_one_add_le`,
     for every sequence of an irreducible `F`;
  3. in characteristic 0, for squarefree `F * G`, the existence of a sequence
     whose last pair has only ordinary multiple points and no common tangents
     (`exists_isQuadraticTransformSeq_forall_not_isTangentAt`), so that 1
     applies to it.

  Its docstring ends with "Milestone: *Noether's formula for intersection
  numbers at infinitely near points*." Parts 1 and 2 hold in every
  characteristic and under weaker hypotheses on the last curves than the book
  asks for (§1). If T5.6 runs long, the milestone moves to a piece of its
  own, about 80 lines.

## Notes

- **Characteristic `p`.** The formula and the inequality (T5.4) hold in every
  characteristic. Only the existence of a sequence for a reducible `FG`
  (T5.6) assumes characteristic 0. For one irreducible curve, T4 gets around a
  terrible centre `P` with an auxiliary step centred at a point `Q` of
  multiplicity `m ∈ {0, 1}`, chosen so that `p ∤ (2n − m) − r`. With several
  components `q_k` for which `P` is terrible, the auxiliary step would need
  `p ∤ (2n_k − m_k) − r_k` for every such `k` at once, where `m_k = m_Q(q_k)`.
  A simple point `Q` of `FG` lies on only one component, so for example in
  characteristic 2, with two such components of even degree, no `Q` of
  multiplicity at most 1 on `FG` works. The book's Problem 7.16 (a) does not
  address this. A route through several auxiliary steps, or through common
  points of the components, may exist, but none was found here. The
  milestone's statements 1 and 2 do not depend on it, and statement 3 is the
  book's construction in the characteristic in which the book argues it
  (Theorem 2 of §7.4 uses Problem 5.26, which is characteristic 0). So the
  design records no `ERRATA.md` entry. A later issue can add characteristic
  `p` by proving the hypothesis of
  `exists_isExcellentPosition_avoiding_of_squarefree` along the sequence.
- **`DESIGN.md`.** T5's row lists B4 among its needs, and this design does not
  use B4. It uses Q13, which the row does not list. A separate `proposed`
  issue asks for the one-line change, as `AGENTS.md` asks. Q13 is built, so the
  order of the parts does not change.
- **Why explicit matrices.** T4's chains (`List.IsChain IsQuadraticTransform`)
  forget the change of coordinates of each step, and with it which points of
  the next curve are over the centre. For one curve, T4 does not need them.
  The formula sums over them, so T5 keeps the matrices.
  `IsQuadraticTransformSeq.isChain` goes back to T4's form. A pair cannot be
  handled as two of T4's chains, because the two chains may use different
  coordinates.
