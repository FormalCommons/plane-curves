## Notation

Everything is in the namespace `PlaneCurves.Projective` unless stated
otherwise, over a field `k`, with `[IsAlgClosed k]` only where points are
needed. `F : MvPolynomial (Fin 3) k` is a form of degree `n`
(`F.IsHomogeneous n`, `F ≠ 0`), and `X 0, X 1, X 2` are Fulton's `X, Y, Z`.

- The **fundamental points** are `fundamentalPoint i := mk k (Pi.single i 1)`:
  Fulton's `P = [0:0:1]` is `fundamentalPoint 2`, `P' = [0:1:0]` is
  `fundamentalPoint 1` and `P'' = [1:0:0]` is `fundamentalPoint 0`. The
  **exceptional line** opposite `fundamentalPoint i` is `V(X i)`: `L = V(Z)`,
  `L' = V(Y)`, `L'' = V(X)`. `fundamentalPoint i` lies on `V(X j)` exactly
  when `i ≠ j`.
- `fundamentalTriangle := zeroLocus {X 0 * X 1 * X 2}`, the union of the
  exceptional lines; Fulton's `U` is its complement, the points with all
  coordinates nonzero.
- `quadraticTransform F = F(YZ, XZ, XY)` is `F^Q` and `properTransform F` is
  `F'`, both from T2 (`QuadraticTransformation/Basic.lean`), with
  `F^Q = X^{r''} Y^{r'} Z^r F'`, `r = mult F (fundamentalPoint 2)`,
  `r' = mult F (fundamentalPoint 1)`, `r'' = mult F (fundamentalPoint 0)`.
- `mult`, `IsSimplePoint`, `IsMultiplePoint`, `IsOrdinaryMultiplePoint`,
  tangents and `intersectionNumber` are the projective ones of Q1–Q3, Q6–Q7;
  `zeroLocus` is `ProjectiveSpace.zeroLocus` (P4). A *line* is a nonzero
  linear form, up to a scalar.
- In this file only, `f := TauCeti.dehomogenize 2 F` (the chart `Z = 1`,
  `P` at the origin) and `F_r := MvPolynomial.leadingFormAt f 0` (L1), the
  tangent cone of `C` at `P`.

## The mathematics, and the route

Let `F` be irreducible in **excellent position**: good position (T2), the
line `L` meets `C = V(F)` transversally in `n` points none of which is
fundamental, and `L'`, `L''` each meet `C` transversally, away from the
fundamental points, in the `n − r` points they have besides `P`. Then `P'`,
`P''` are not on `C`, so `r' = r'' = 0` and `F^Q = Z^r F'`, and
`deg F' = 2n − r` (T2). The milestone describes the multiple points of
`C' = V(F')` in four groups:

1. **Off the fundamental triangle.** On `U` the map
   `Q[x:y:z] = [yz:xz:xy]` is an involution and `F'(v) = 0 ↔ F(Qv) = 0`. In
   the chart `Z = 1` it is `(x, y) ↦ (1/x, 1/y)`, an automorphism of
   `k[x, y][1/(xy)]` that sends `f` to a unit times `dehomogenize 2 F'`.
   So it gives an isomorphism of the local rings
   `𝒪_R(𝔸²)/(f) ≅ 𝒪_{Q(R)}(𝔸²)/(f')` of the two curves, for `R ∈ U`. Multiplicity (L5,
   **3-ring**) and being an ordinary multiple point (L12, **3-ordinary**)
   depend only on that ring. There is **no linear change of coordinates**
   relating `C` and `C'` there, so this route through the local ring cannot
   be avoided. Excellent position makes every point of `C` on an exceptional
   line other than `P` a simple point (its intersection number with that
   line is 1). So these are all the multiple points of `C` other than `P`.
2. **The fundamental points.** `mult F' P = n`, `mult F' P' = mult F' P'' = n − r`
   (T2). The leading forms of `F'` there are, up to swapping the
   variables, the restrictions of `F` to `L`, `L'` and `L''` with the factor
   `X^r` (resp. `Y^r`) at `P` removed. Excellent position makes them
   squarefree. So `P`, `P'`, `P''` are ordinary when their multiplicity is at
   least 2.
3. **On `L'` and `L''`.** `F'(X, 0, Z) = c Z^{n−r} X^n` with
   `c = F(0, 1, 0) ≠ 0`, and symmetrically for `L''`. So `C'` meets `L'`,
   `L''` only in fundamental points.
4. **On `L`, the points over the centre.** `F'(X, Y, 0) = F_r(Y, X)(XY)^{n−r}`,
   so the non-fundamental points of `C' ∩ L` are the `[α : 1 : 0]` with
   `F_r(1, α) = 0`, `α ≠ 0`. Good position (neither `X` nor `Y` is tangent
   at `P`) makes every tangent direction of `C` at `P` one of them. `m ≤ I` is
   **3-prodmult** with the line. `∑ I(P_i, C' ∩ L) = r` follows from Bézout
   (Q12) for `F'` and `Z`: the two fundamental points on `L` contribute
   `n − r` each, because `Z` is not tangent to `C'` there (good position of
   `C'`, T2). In the chart `Y = 1`, with `f' = dehomogenize 1 F'`, there is
   the polynomial identity (Step (5))

   ```
   f'(x, x z) = x^{n−r} · g(z, x),      g := blowupStrictTransform f   (B1)
   ```

   Since `(x, z) ↦ (x, xz)` is an automorphism of `k[x, z][1/x]`, it
   identifies `𝒪_{P_i}(C')` with the local ring of the blow-up `g` at
   `(0, α)`: near `P` the quadratic transformation *is* the blow-up of §7.2.
   So B1's part (2) applies. At an ordinary point of `C`, a simple point or
   a point off `C` (the atlas's (4)), all `P_i` are simple.

`g*` (Step (8)) is then arithmetic, and the existence of excellent position
(Lemma 1) is the choice of three lines by part (d) of **7-terrible** (T1),
moved to the coordinate lines by P9.

**Characteristic.** Lemma 1 is false in characteristic `p` at a terrible
point (Appendix A). As the atlas's formalization notes recommend, T3 states
existence for every point that is **not terrible**, in any characteristic.
The book's form (characteristic 0, every point) is a corollary, since no
point is terrible in characteristic 0 (part (b) of **7-terrible**). Parts
(2)–(4) hold in every characteristic.

## Definitions and their API

### Fundamental points (in `Position.lean`, unless T2 already has them)

T2 (#111) needs the fundamental points to state **7-quadratic**. If T2's
`Basic.lean` defines them, T3 uses those and adds the missing API to
`Basic.lean`.

- `fundamentalPoint (i : Fin 3) : ℙ k (Fin 3 → k)`, `mk k (Pi.single i 1) _`.
  API: `fundamentalPoint_injective`, `fundamentalPoint_mem_zeroLocus_X_iff`
  (`fundamentalPoint i ∈ zeroLocus {X j} ↔ i ≠ j`),
  `mult_fundamentalPoint_eq_orderAt` (`= orderAt (dehomogenize i F) 0`).
- `fundamentalTriangle : Set (ℙ k (Fin 3 → k))`, `zeroLocus {X 0 * X 1 * X 2}`.
  API: `mem_fundamentalTriangle_iff` (`∃ i, P.rep i = 0`, and the `_mk` form),
  `fundamentalPoint_mem_fundamentalTriangle`.

### The point map (in `QuadraticTransformation/Basic.lean`, unless T2 has it)

- `quadraticMap (P : ℙ k (Fin 3 → k)) : ℙ k (Fin 3 → k)`: `[yz : xz : xy]` at
  `P.rep = (x, y, z)` when that vector is nonzero (off the fundamental
  points), and `P` otherwise (junk value). API: `quadraticMap_mk` (for every
  representative with at most one zero coordinate), `quadraticMap_quadraticMap`
  (on `fundamentalTriangleᶜ`), `quadraticMap_mem_fundamentalTriangle_iff`,
  `quadraticMap_mem_zeroLocus_properTransform_iff`
  (`P ∉ fundamentalTriangle → (quadraticMap P ∈ zeroLocus {properTransform F} ↔ P ∈ zeroLocus {F})`),
  `bijOn_quadraticMap` (from `zeroLocus {F} \ fundamentalTriangle` onto
  `zeroLocus {properTransform F} \ fundamentalTriangle`).

### Excellent position (`Position.lean`)

- `IsExcellentPosition (F : MvPolynomial (Fin 3) k) : Prop`, the conjunction of
  1. `IsGoodPosition F` (T2);
  2. `∀ j, ∀ Q ∈ zeroLocus {F, X j}, Q ≠ fundamentalPoint 2 → (∀ i, Q ≠ fundamentalPoint i) ∧ intersectionNumber Q F (X j) = 1`.

  For `j = 2` the hypothesis `Q ≠ fundamentalPoint 2` always holds, since
  `P` is not on `L`, so this is the conjunction of the conditions on `L` and
  on `L'`, `L''`. This is Fulton's definition (p. 90), stated pointwise. The
  counts `n` and `n − r` are consequences (Bézout), not part of the
  definition, so the definition needs no `ncard`. API:
  - `IsExcellentPosition.smul` (`c ≠ 0`), and `isExcellentPosition_smul_iff`;
  - `IsExcellentPosition.fundamentalPoint_notMem` (`i ≠ 2`: `P'`, `P''` are
    not on `C`), `IsExcellentPosition.eval_single_ne_zero` (`F(eᵢ) ≠ 0`) and
    `IsExcellentPosition.mult_fundamentalPoint_eq_zero` for `i ≠ 2`;
  - `IsExcellentPosition.not_X_dvd` (no exceptional line divides `F`), so
    T2's results apply;
  - `IsExcellentPosition.intersectionNumber_eq_one`,
    `intersectionNumber_X_two_eq_one`, `intersectionNumber_le_one`,
    `intersectionNumber_fundamentalPoint_two` (`I(P, F ∩ Xⱼ) = m_P(F)` for
    `j ≠ 2`, by good position);
  - `IsExcellentPosition.isSimplePoint_of_mem_fundamentalTriangle`
    (`Q ∈ zeroLocus {F} ∩ fundamentalTriangle`, `Q ≠ fundamentalPoint 2` →
    `IsSimplePoint F Q`; by **3-prodmult** with a line);
    `IsExcellentPosition.notMem_fundamentalTriangle_of_isMultiplePoint`;
  - the squarefree restrictions, for `[IsAlgClosed k]`:
    `IsExcellentPosition.squarefree_restrict_X_two`: the binary form
    `aeval ![X 0, X 1, 0] F` is squarefree and divisible by neither `X 0`
    nor `X 1`; `IsExcellentPosition.restrict_X_one_eq`:
    `aeval ![X 0, 0, X 2] F = X 0 ^ r * H` with `H` squarefree and divisible
    by neither `X 0` nor `X 2`; `IsExcellentPosition.restrict_X_zero_eq`, the
    same for `aeval ![0, X 1, X 2] F` and `X 1`; both from
    `IsExcellentPosition.exists_restrict_X_eq`, stated with
    `bind₁ (Function.update X j 0)`. The proof uses intersection numbers
    only: `squarefree_of_forall_intersectionNumber_le_one` (in
    `Projective/GenericLine.lean`) says that a nonzero form meeting a curve of
    positive degree with all intersection numbers at most 1 is squarefree,
    since a square factor `p²` meets the curve at some point, by the
    projective dimension theorem. The restriction has the same intersection
    numbers with `V(Xⱼ)` as `F` (`intersectionNumber_bind₁_update_X_zero`).
    The factor `X_l ^ r` comes from T2's
    `mult_fundamentalPoint_le_multiplicity_bind₁`.
  - `IsExcellentPosition.ncard_zeroLocus_X_two` (`= n`) and
    `ncard_zeroLocus_X_diff` (`= n − r` for `j ≠ 2`), by Bézout (Q12).
    These are for the statement in Fulton's form; nothing in T3 needs them.
- `IsQuadraticTransformAt (F : MvPolynomial (Fin 3) k) (P : ℙ k (Fin 3 → k)) (G : MvPolynomial (Fin 3) k) : Prop`:
  `∃ M : Matrix (Fin 3) (Fin 3) k, IsUnit M.det ∧ Projectivization.map (Matrix.mulVecLin M) _ (fundamentalPoint 2) = P ∧ IsExcellentPosition (linearSubst M F) ∧ G = properTransform (linearSubst M F)`.
  This is Fulton's "a quadratic transformation of `F` centred at `P`". `F^T`
  is `linearSubst M F`, with `eval x (linearSubst M F) = eval (M *ᵥ x) F`
  (Tau Ceti `MvPolynomial.aeval_linearSubst`), so `T = M`. T4 iterates
  it. API: `IsQuadraticTransformAt.isHomogeneous` (degree `2n − r`,
  `r = mult F P`), `IsQuadraticTransformAt.irreducible`, and the existence
  theorem below.

### Points over the centre (`Singularities/Basic.lean`)

- `pointsOverCentre (F : MvPolynomial (Fin 3) k) : Set (ℙ k (Fin 3 → k))`:
  `{Q | Q ∈ zeroLocus {properTransform F, X 2} ∧ ∀ i, Q ≠ fundamentalPoint i}`,
  the points `P_1, …, P_s` of `C' ∩ L` that are not fundamental. Fulton's
  "neighbouring singularities" (Problem 7.15, T5) are the multiple ones among
  them. API, under `IsExcellentPosition F` and `[IsAlgClosed k]`:
  `pointsOverCentre_finite`; `mem_pointsOverCentre_iff`
  (`Q ∈ pointsOverCentre F ↔ ∃ α ≠ 0, Q = mk k ![α, 1, 0] _ ∧ eval ![1, α] F_r = 0`);
  `pointsOverCentre_subset_fundamentalTriangle`.

### The virtual genus (`Singularities/Genus.lean`)

- `virtualGenus (F : MvPolynomial (Fin 3) k) : ℤ`, as `DESIGN.md` gives it:
  `((F.totalDegree - 1).choose 2 : ℤ) - ∑ᶠ P, ((mult F P).toNat.choose 2 : ℤ)`,
  that is `(n−1)(n−2)/2 − Σ_P m_P(m_P − 1)/2` for `n ≥ 1`. The `finsum` is
  over all points. Only multiple points contribute, and for irreducible `F`
  there are finitely many (L3, through the chart). The value is junk when
  they are infinitely many, that is when `F` has a multiple component;
  every statement here assumes `F` irreducible. `Nat.choose · 2` avoids
  division (`Nat.choose_two_right` relates the two). API:
  - `virtualGenus_def`; `two_mul_virtualGenus`
    (`2 * g* = (n−1)(n−2) − Σᶠ P, m_P (m_P − 1)` in `ℤ`, for `n ≥ 1`), the
    form used in computations with `ring` and `linarith`;
  - `virtualGenus_smul` (`c ≠ 0`); `virtualGenus_linearSubst` (`IsUnit M.det`:
    the points move by `Projectivization.map (Matrix.mulVecLin M)`, a
    bijection, with `mult_linearSubst` (Q1) and `finsum_comp_equiv`);
  - `virtualGenus_nonneg` (`Irreducible F`, `[IsAlgClosed k]`), which is
    **5-bound** (Q14) restated;
  - `virtualGenus_eq_of_isNonsingular` (`= (n−1)(n−2)/2`), and a line or a
    conic has `g* = 0`, as examples.

## Main results

All in `PlaneCurves.Projective`, over `[IsAlgClosed k]`, for
`F` irreducible and homogeneous of degree `n` and `r = (mult F (fundamentalPoint 2)).toNat`,
unless stated.

### Existence (`Position.lean`; Lemma 1, Problem 7.14 (a), Appendix A)

- `exists_isExcellentPosition (hP : ¬ IsTerriblePoint F P.rep)`:
  `∃ M, IsUnit M.det ∧ Projectivization.map (Matrix.mulVecLin M) _ (fundamentalPoint 2) = P ∧ IsExcellentPosition (linearSubst M F)`,
  for every point `P` of `ℙ²`, on `C` or not.
- `exists_isQuadraticTransformAt` (the same hypothesis):
  `∃ G, IsQuadraticTransformAt F P G`.
- `exists_isExcellentPosition_of_charZero` (`[CharZero k]`, every `P`), the
  book's Lemma 1, from part (b) of **7-terrible**.

### Singularities of `C'` (`Singularities/Basic.lean`, `Chart.lean`; Steps (5)–(7))

For `IsExcellentPosition F`, with `F' = properTransform F`:

- `IsExcellentPosition.quadraticTransform_eq` (`F^Q = X 2 ^ r * F'`) and
  `IsExcellentPosition.isHomogeneous_properTransform` (degree `2n − r`).
- Restrictions to the exceptional lines (no `IsAlgClosed`):
  `aeval_X2_eq_zero_properTransform`: `aeval ![X 0, X 1, 0] F' = (X 0 * X 1) ^ (n − r) * rename ![1, 0] F_r`,
  where `rename ![1, 0]` sends the two variables of `F_r` to `X 1`, `X 0` (Fulton's
  `F_r(Y, X)`); `aeval_X1_eq_zero_properTransform`:
  `aeval ![X 0, 0, X 2] F' = C (eval ![0, 1, 0] F) * X 0 ^ n * X 2 ^ (n − r)`;
  and the same for `X 0`. Consequences:
  `zeroLocus_properTransform_X1_subset` (`⊆ {fundamentalPoint 0, fundamentalPoint 2}`),
  `zeroLocus_properTransform_X0_subset` (`⊆ {fundamentalPoint 1, fundamentalPoint 2}`),
  `mem_zeroLocus_properTransform_iff_of_mem_fundamentalTriangle` (the points
  of `C'` on the triangle are fundamental or over the centre).
- (2)(a), **the points off the triangle**, for any `F` with no exceptional
  line as a component (excellent position is not needed):
  `mult_properTransform_quadraticMap` (`Q ∉ fundamentalTriangle →
  mult F' (quadraticMap Q) = mult F Q`) and
  `isOrdinaryMultiplePoint_properTransform_quadraticMap_iff`. Both come
  from `localRingEquivQuadraticMap` (below) with L5 and L12.
- (2)(b), **the fundamental points**: `IsExcellentPosition.mult_properTransform_fundamentalPoint`
  (`n`, `n − r`, `n − r` at `P`, `P'`, `P''`; from T2 with `r' = r'' = 0`),
  `IsExcellentPosition.squarefree_leadingFormAt_properTransform` (at each
  fundamental point, in its chart), hence
  `IsExcellentPosition.isOrdinaryMultiplePoint_properTransform_fundamentalPoint`
  (when the multiplicity is at least 2). The leading forms are computed
  explicitly: at `P` it is `F(Y, X, 0)` in the chart `Z = 1`, and at `P'` it
  is `H(Z, X)`, where `F(X, 0, Z) = X^r H(X, Z)`. They are squarefree by
  `IsExcellentPosition.squarefree_restrict_X_two` and `restrict_X_one_eq`.
- (2)(c), **the points over the centre**:
  `IsExcellentPosition.mult_le_intersectionNumber_of_mem_pointsOverCentre`
  (`mult F' Q ≤ intersectionNumber Q F' (X 2)`, by **3-prodmult**) and
  `IsExcellentPosition.finsum_intersectionNumber_pointsOverCentre`
  (`∑ᶠ Q ∈ pointsOverCentre F, intersectionNumber Q F' (X 2) = r`, by
  Bézout (Q12) and the tangency criterion at `P'`, `P''`).
- **Near `P` the map is the blow-up** (Step (5)):
  `dehomogenize_properTransform_blowup`
  (`aeval ![X 0, X 0 * X 1] (dehomogenize 1 F') = X 0 ^ (n − r) * rename (Equiv.swap 0 1) (blowupStrictTransform f)`,
  a polynomial identity); `localRingEquivPointsOverCentre` (for `α ≠ 0`, the
  local ring of `dehomogenize 1 F'` at `![α, 0]` is isomorphic to that of
  `blowupStrictTransform f` at `![0, α]`); hence
  `mult_properTransform_mk_eq_orderAt_blowupStrictTransform` and
  `IsExcellentPosition.isSimplePoint_of_mem_pointsOverCentre` when
  `r ≤ 1` or `IsOrdinaryMultiplePoint F (fundamentalPoint 2)`, from part (2)
  of **7-blowup** (B1). The hypothesis of B1, that `X` is not tangent at the
  origin, is part of good position.
- **All the multiple points**:
  `IsExcellentPosition.isMultiplePoint_properTransform_iff`:
  `IsMultiplePoint F' Q ↔ (Q ∉ fundamentalTriangle ∧ IsMultiplePoint F (quadraticMap Q)) ∨ (∃ i, Q = fundamentalPoint i ∧ IsMultiplePoint F' Q) ∨ (Q ∈ pointsOverCentre F ∧ IsMultiplePoint F' Q)`,
  with the multiplicities above.

### `g*` (`Singularities/Genus.lean`; Step (8), Problem 7.14 (a))

- `IsExcellentPosition.virtualGenus_properTransform`:
  `virtualGenus F' = virtualGenus F − ∑ᶠ Q ∈ pointsOverCentre F, ((mult F' Q).toNat.choose 2 : ℤ)`.
  Proof: split both `finsum`s over the triangle and its complement with
  `finsum_mem_union'`, not `finsum_mem_union`: the latter needs both sets
  finite, and the triangle and its complement are infinite. The hypotheses
  of `finsum_mem_union'` are that the two sets are disjoint and that each
  meets the support of `fun Q ↦ ((mult F Q).toNat.choose 2 : ℤ)`, which is
  the set of multiple points of `F`, in a finite set. Both follow from the
  finiteness of the multiple points of an irreducible curve (L3, through the
  affine charts), for `F` and likewise for `F'`. Then match the complements by `bijOn_quadraticMap`
  (`finsum_mem_image`) with equal multiplicities, and compute the rest with
  `two_mul_virtualGenus` and `ring`.
- `IsQuadraticTransformAt.virtualGenus` (the same through `linearSubst`) and
  `IsQuadraticTransformAt.virtualGenus_eq` (the atlas's (4)): if
  `mult F P ≤ 1` or `IsOrdinaryMultiplePoint F P`, then `virtualGenus G = virtualGenus F`
  and `G` has degree `2n − r`, that is `2n − r`, `2n − 1` or `2n`.

### The milestone (`Singularities/Genus.lean`)

- `quadraticTransform_centre_singularities`: one theorem, the conjunction of
  1. `exists_isQuadraticTransformAt` for every point that is not terrible;
  2. for `IsExcellentPosition F`: the degree `2n − r`, the description
     `isMultiplePoint_properTransform_iff` with the multiplicities and
     ordinariness of (2)(a)–(b), and (2)(c);
  3. `virtualGenus_properTransform`;
  4. `IsQuadraticTransformAt.virtualGenus_eq`.

  Its docstring ends with "Milestone: *Quadratic transformation centred at a
  point and its new singularities*." Part 1 is more general than the
  atlas's (1) (non-terrible points in any characteristic, not every point in
  characteristic 0), so it implies it. The theorem may be stated with the
  CharZero corollary as a separate, named conjunct, so that the plan's form
  appears literally. The milestone's explicit open isomorphisms near `P` are
  `dehomogenize_properTransform_blowup` and `localRingEquivPointsOverCentre`.

### T3.1 — Local-ring isomorphisms from localizations away from a polynomial (~250 lines)

File: `AffineSpace/LocalRing.lean` (A9), and the L5 and L12 files for the
two transfer lemmas if those parts have not stated them this way.

- `AffineSpace.localRingEquivOfAway (e : Localization.Away h ≃ₐ[k] Localization.Away h') (hP : eval P h ≠ 0) (hQ : eval Q h' ≠ 0) (he : (Localization.awayLift (eval Q) h' _).comp e = Localization.awayLift (eval P) h _) : AffineSpace.localRing k P ≃ₐ[k] AffineSpace.localRing k Q`.
  `localRing k P` is the localization of `Away h` at the kernel of
  evaluation at `P`, and `e` maps that prime to the one of `Q`.
  API: `localRingEquivOfAway_algebraMap` (on the image of `Away h`),
  `localRingEquivOfAway_symm`, `evalAt_localRingEquivOfAway`.
- `AffineSpace.quotientEquivOfAway`: if moreover
  `e (algebraMap f) = u * algebraMap g` with `u` a unit, then
  `(localRing k P ⧸ span {f}) ≃ₐ[k] (localRing k Q ⧸ span {g})`
  (`Ideal.quotientEquivAlg`).
- `Affine.orderAt_eq_of_ringEquiv` and
  `Affine.isOrdinaryMultiplePoint_iff_of_ringEquiv` for such ring
  isomorphisms of `𝒪_P(𝔸²)/(F)`, `𝒪_Q(𝔸²)/(G)` (`F`, `G` irreducible, or in
  the generality L5 and L12 prove), unless **3-ring** and **3-ordinary**
  already are these statements.
- Test: the translation `taylor` and `linearSubst` isomorphisms of A9 are
  instances (with `h = 1`), or an example with `h = X 0`.

Needs: A9, L5, L12.

### T3.3 — Existence of excellent position (~250 lines)

File: `Position.lean`.

`exists_isExcellentPosition`, `IsQuadraticTransformAt` with its API,
`exists_isQuadraticTransformAt`, `exists_isExcellentPosition_of_charZero`.
Proof: by part (d) of **7-terrible**, choose distinct lines `ℓ'`, `ℓ''`
through `P`, each meeting `C` in `n − r` further points transversally with
`I(P, C ∩ ℓ) = r` (so not tangent at `P`, by the tangency criterion). Then
choose `ℓ` not through `P` and avoiding the finite set `C ∩ (ℓ' ∪ ℓ'')`,
meeting `C` transversally in `n` points. Move `ℓ'', ℓ', ℓ` to
`V(X), V(Y), V(Z)` by P9's Problem 4.16. For three linear forms this is the
invertibility of their coefficient matrix `A`, and `M = A⁻¹` gives
`linearSubst M ℓ ∝ X 2`, …, so the piece may state the needed lemma for
linear forms directly if P9's statement, in terms of hyperplanes of
`Projectivization`, is harder to use. Transport the conditions with the
`linearSubst` invariance of `mult`, `intersectionNumber`, tangency and
`zeroLocus`.

Needs: T3.2, T1, P9.

## Notes

- **No errata.** The atlas already records the two problems of the source
  that T3 meets: Lemma 1 fails at terrible points in characteristic `p`
  (followed here by assuming "not terrible"), and Step (7) (b) calls `P`, `P'`, `P''`
  ordinary multiple points even when their multiplicity is at most 1
  (followed here by stating ordinariness only for multiplicity at least 2).
  T3 adds no entry to `ERRATA.md`.
- **Asymmetry.** Excellent position is not symmetric in the three
  fundamental points (the atlas's note); `IsExcellentPosition` singles out
  `fundamentalPoint 2` as the centre, as Fulton does.
- **Why not compare `C` and `C'` by a change of coordinates.** Off the
  triangle the map `Q` is not linear, so `mult_linearSubst` and the
  tangent-form API do not apply. The only route is the one the atlas
  prescribes: an isomorphism of local rings (T3.1), with **3-ring** and
  **3-ordinary**. T3.1 builds it once, for any automorphism of a
  localization `k[X][1/h]` compatible with evaluation; T3.5 and T3.6 use it
  for the inversion and for the blow-up chart. T5 (Noether's formula, global
  form) will use the same isomorphisms for intersection numbers.
- **For T4.** T4 iterates `IsQuadraticTransformAt` with a non-ordinary
  multiple point as centre, and in characteristic `p` with an auxiliary
  centre of multiplicity 0 or 1 that is not terrible. It needs exactly:
  `exists_isQuadraticTransformAt`, `IsQuadraticTransformAt.irreducible`,
  the degree, `isMultiplePoint_properTransform_iff` transported through
  `linearSubst`, `IsQuadraticTransformAt.virtualGenus`,
  `IsQuadraticTransformAt.virtualGenus_eq` and `virtualGenus_nonneg`. The
  centre `P` itself is replaced by the point `fundamentalPoint 2` of `C'`,
  ordinary of multiplicity `n`, and by the points over the centre; that is
  part of `isMultiplePoint_properTransform_iff`. Every one of these is listed
  above.
