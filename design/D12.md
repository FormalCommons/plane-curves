milestone **8-canonical** (*Canonical divisors of a plane curve are cut out

## Approach

Throughout, `k` is algebraically closed and `F : MvPolynomial (Fin 3) k` is an
irreducible form of degree `n` (`[Fact (Irreducible F)]`, as Q5 asks) whose
multiple points are all ordinary. `K = functionField F` (Q5) has
`hK : TauCeti.IsFunctionField k K` (Q5) and
`hex : IsIntegrallyClosedIn k K` (Tau Ceti's `isIntegrallyClosedIn_of_isAlgClosed`,
`TauCeti/RingTheory/IntegralClosure/IntegrallyClosed.lean`). `E` is
`adjointDivisor F` (D5), `div G` is `formDivisor F G` (D4), and `V_m` is
`adjointSubmodule F m` (D5).

A divisor `W` is *canonical* if
`(Place.orderSystem hK).divisorClass W = canonicalClass hK hex`. This is Tau
Ceti's canonical class, defined by Weil differentials. D1 shows that it is
exactly the class of the divisors `kaehlerDifferentialDivisor ω` of the Kähler
differentials `ω ≠ 0`, which are the book's canonical divisors. So there is no
new predicate, and every statement can be turned into the Kähler form with
D1's API.

The atlas's route (Fulton's) proves the explicit formula
`div(dx/f_y) = (n − 3) div(Z) − E` place by place, as an equality. It needs
upper bounds on orders of forms: `ord(F_X) = r − 1` at places over an `r`-fold
point (Problem 7.4, B3 (3)) and `F_X ≠ 0` at the points of `Z ∩ C`
(Problem 5.16). This design takes a shorter route that needs only lower
bounds, which D5 and B3 give, and degrees:

- **(a) without coordinates.** Tau Ceti's characterization
  `TauCeti.divisorClass_eq_canonicalClass_iff` (Stichtenoth 1.6.2) says that
  `D` is canonical iff `deg D = 2g − 2` and `g ≤ ℓ(D)`. For
  `E_{n−3} = (n − 3) div L − E`, with `L` linear and `F ∤ L`, both hold, and
  both are part of D9's milestone (8-genus), which states
  `deg E_{n−3} = 2g − 2` and `l(E_{n−3}) ≥ g` for every `n ≥ 1`. This part
  uses those two statements of D9 as they are and does not restate them. So
  `E_{n−3}` is canonical for every `n ≥ 1`. That is the atlas's "natural general form". For a form `G` of
  degree `n − 3` with `F ∤ G`, `div G − E` is linearly equivalent to it,
  because `div G − (n − 3) div L` is principal (D4's `principal_formQuotient`).
- **The explicit formula.** In the atlas's coordinates (the predicate
  `IsTransversePosition` below) and for `n ≥ 2`, let
  `ω₀ = f_y⁻¹ • dx ∈ Ω[K⁄k]`, with `x = X/Z`, `y = Y/Z`, `f_x = F_X/Z^{n−1}`
  and `f_y = F_Y/Z^{n−1}`. Differentiating `F(x, y, 1) = 0` gives
  `f_x dx + f_y dy = 0`, so `ω₀ = −f_x⁻¹ • dy` as well. At every place `P`:
  - *over an affine multiple point* `Q = [a : b : 1]` of multiplicity `r`:
    the line `Y − bZ` passes through `[1 : 0 : 0]`, so it is not tangent at
    `Q`, and B3 gives `ord_P(y − b) = 1`. Hence `ord_P(dy) = 0` (D1). Also
    `m_Q(F_X) ≥ r − 1` (Tau Ceti's `MvPolynomial.succ_le_orderAt_iff`), so
    `ord_P(f_x) ≥ r − 1` (B3 (1)). So
    `ord_P(ω₀) = ord_P(dy) − ord_P(f_x) ≤ −(r − 1) = −E_P`;
  - *over an affine simple point* `Q = [a : b : 1]`: one of `X − aZ` and
    `Y − bZ` is not the tangent, so it has order 1 at the only place over `Q`.
    Then `dx` or `dy` has order 0, and `f_x`, `f_y` have order `≥ 0`, so
    `ord_P(ω₀) ≤ 0 = −E_P`;
  - *over a point* `Q = [a : 1 : 0]` *of* `Z ∩ C`: `I(Q, C ∩ Z) = 1`, so `Q` is
    simple and `ord_P(Z) = 1`. Hence `u = Z/Y` is a uniformizer,
    `y = u⁻¹`, `ord_P(dy) = −2`, and `ord_P(f_x) ≥ −(n − 1)`. So
    `ord_P(ω₀) ≤ n − 3 = (n − 3) ord_P(Z) − E_P`.

  So `div ω₀ ≤ (n − 3) div Z − E`. Both sides have degree `2g − 2`: the left
  side because it is canonical (D1), the right side by D9 (with `L = Z`).
  A difference that is effective and has degree 0 is zero (Tau Ceti's
  `TauCeti.Divisor.degree_eq_zero_iff`), so the two are equal. Problems 5.16
  and 7.4 are not needed.
- **(b).** For a canonical `W`, `deg W = 2g − 2` and `ℓ(W) = g`
  (`TauCeti.isRiemannRochDivisor_of_divisorClass_eq_canonicalClass` with
  `IsRiemannRochDivisor.degree_eq` and `.dim_eq`). This is stronger than the
  book's `ℓ(W) ≥ g`. Let `n ≥ 3`, and let `W ≥ 0` be canonical. Then
  `W = E_{n−3} + div h` with `h ∈ L(E_{n−3})`, `h ≠ 0`. By D8, `h = G/L^{n−3}`
  for an adjoint `G` of degree `n − 3`, so `W = div G − E`. The converse is
  D5's `isAdjoint_iff_le_formDivisor` together with (a). Since `n − 3 < n`,
  D8 gives `V_{n−3} ≅ L(E_{n−3})`, so `dim V_{n−3} = ℓ(E_{n−3}) = g`. The
  holomorphic differentials are the `h • ω₀` with
  `h ∈ L((n − 3) div Z − E)`, because `div(h • ω₀) = div h + div ω₀` (D1).
  By D8 these `h` are the `H/Z^{n−3}`, `H ∈ V_{n−3}`, which are the
  `H(x, y, 1)`.

Tau Ceti proves the nonsingular case (`E = 0`):
`TauCeti.divisorClass_sub_three_zsmul_sup_poles_eq_canonicalClass_of_isHomogeneous`
and `TauCeti.riemannRochSpace_sub_three_zsmul_sup_poles_eq_span_of_isHomogeneous`
(`TauCeti/FieldTheory/FunctionField/PlaneCurve/Projective.lean`), and the
affine form in `.../PlaneCurve/RegularDifferential.lean`. Its proof uses
Dedekind's different formula for a smooth model
(`TauCeti.Divisor.different_eq_principal_add_zsmul_poles`), which fails at
singular points, so it does not extend to `E ≠ 0`. The part reproves none of
it. Its results are more general, since they hold for curves with ordinary
multiple points, and they agree with Tau Ceti's when `E = 0` (D5).

**Characteristic.** Nothing depends on the characteristic. The coordinates
need `F_X ≠ 0` and `F_Y ≠ 0`. For `n ≥ 2` these follow from the position, as
the atlas's formalization notes say. `F(X, 1, 0)` has `n` distinct roots and
degree `n`, so its derivative `F_X(X, 1, 0)` is nonzero. `F(1, Y, 0)` has
distinct roots and degree `≥ n − 1 ≥ 1`, so `F_Y(1, Y, 0)` is nonzero. For
`n = 1` the explicit formula is meaningless: for `F = X − cZ` the position
holds, but `F_Y = 0` and `x = c` is constant, so `dx = 0`. Hence `2 ≤ n` in
that statement. The atlas states (a) for forms of degree `n − 3`, which
presupposes `n ≥ 3`, so this is no departure and needs no `ERRATA.md` entry.

### `CanonicalDivisor/Basic.lean`

No definitions.

- `divisorClass_sub_three_zsmul_formDivisor_sub_adjointDivisor_eq_canonicalClass`:
  `E_{n−3}` is canonical, for every linear `L` with `F ∤ L`, from D9's two
  statements `deg E_{n−3} = 2g − 2` and `g ≤ ℓ(E_{n−3})` and
  `TauCeti.divisorClass_eq_canonicalClass_iff`. Its name follows Tau Ceti's
  nonsingular theorem. D9's milestone states both for every `n ≥ 1` and every
  linear `L` with `F ∤ L`; use them under the names D9's pull request gives
  them. If D9 proves them in less generality (only for `n ≥ 3`, or only for
  one `L`), generalize them in D9's file (`PlaneCurve/Genus/Basic.lean`)
  instead of restating them here.
- `divisorClass_formDivisor_sub_adjointDivisor_eq_canonicalClass`: for a
  form `G` of degree `n − 3` with `F ∤ G` (`n ≥ 3`), `div G − E` is
  canonical. Use `divisorClass` of `principal_formQuotient F G (L ^ (n − 3))`
  (D4) and the previous lemma. For `n = 3`, `G` is a nonzero constant and
  `-E` is canonical (`formDivisor` of a constant is 0, D4); state this as
  `divisorClass_neg_adjointDivisor_eq_canonicalClass`.
- `exists_kaehlerDifferentialDivisor_eq_formDivisor_sub_adjointDivisor`: the
  same in the book's form, with `ω : Ω[K⁄k]`, `ω ≠ 0`, and
  `kaehlerDifferentialDivisor ω _ = div G − E`, by D1's statement that every
  divisor of the canonical class is the divisor of a Kähler differential.
  If D1 does not state that, add it to D1's file
  (`FieldTheory/FunctionField/KaehlerDifferential.lean`). It follows from
  `TauCeti.exists_isGreatest_of_divisorClass_eq_canonicalClass` and the
  comparison `TauCeti.kaehlerDifferentialEquivWeilDifferentialOfSeparating`.
- `degree_eq_of_divisorClass_eq_canonicalClass` and
  `dim_eq_of_divisorClass_eq_canonicalClass`: `deg W = 2g − 2` and
  `ℓ(W) = g`. Use them only if `TauCeti.isRiemannRochDivisor_of_divisorClass_eq_canonicalClass`
  followed by `Divisor.IsRiemannRochDivisor.degree_eq` and `.dim_eq` is not
  short enough to use inline. They are not about plane curves, so if they are
  added, they go in D1's file.
- `nonneg_and_divisorClass_eq_canonicalClass_iff` (`n ≥ 3`):
  `0 ≤ W ∧ divisorClass W = canonicalClass hK hex ↔ ∃ G ∈ adjointSubmodule F (n - 3), G ≠ 0 ∧ W = formDivisor F G - adjointDivisor F`.
  Here `F ∤ G` because `G ≠ 0` and `deg G < n`
  (`MvPolynomial.totalDegree_le_of_dvd_of_isDomain`). Forward: linear
  equivalence gives a unit `z` with `W = E_{n−3} + principal z`, so `z` is in
  `riemannRochSpace E_{n−3}`. D8's surjectivity writes `z = G/L^{n−3}`. Then
  apply `principal_formQuotient`. Backward: D5's
  `isAdjoint_iff_le_formDivisor` and the previous lemma.
- `finrank_adjointSubmodule_sub_three` (`n ≥ 3`):
  `Module.finrank k (adjointSubmodule F (n - 3)) = genus k K`. D8 gives the
  isomorphism `V_m ≅ L(E_m)` for `m < n` (the kernel, the multiples of `F`, is
  0 there). The canonical `E_{n−3}` has `ℓ = g`.

### `CanonicalDivisor/Position.lean`

- **`structure IsTransversePosition (F : MvPolynomial (Fin 3) k) : Prop`**,
  with fields:
  - `intersectionNumber_X_two`: `∀ P ∈ zeroLocus {F, X 2}, intersectionNumber P F (X 2) = 1`
    (Q6). The line `Z` meets `C` transversally, so in `n` distinct points by
    Bézout. This is the form that T1 (d) gives;
  - `eval_ne_zero`: `eval ![1, 0, 0] F ≠ 0`, that is, `[1 : 0 : 0] ∉ C`;
  - `not_isTangentAt`: no tangent at a multiple point passes through
    `[1 : 0 : 0]`. That is, for every multiple point `P` and every tangent
    line `L` at `P` (Q3's projective tangent predicate),
    `eval ![1, 0, 0] L ≠ 0`.

  Fulton's "excellent position" (T3) is a different set of conditions, so
  this one gets its own name. API:
  - `IsTransversePosition.isSimplePoint`: the points of `Z ∩ C` are simple
    (`mul_mult_le_intersectionNumber`, property (5), Q7);
  - `IsTransversePosition.eval_one_ne_zero`: every point of `Z ∩ C` is
    `[a : 1 : 0]`;
  - `IsTransversePosition.pderiv_zero_ne_zero` and `.pderiv_one_ne_zero`
    (`2 ≤ n` for the second): `F_X ≠ 0` and `F_Y ≠ 0`, by the argument under
    *Characteristic* above. The binary forms come from A4, and the
    distinctness of the roots from the field `intersectionNumber_X_two` and
    L10's intersection number with a line;
  - `IsTransversePosition.not_dvd_pderiv_zero` and `.not_dvd_pderiv_one`:
    `F ∤ F_X` and `F ∤ F_Y` (degree `n − 1 < n`);
  - `IsTransversePosition.not_dvd_X` (`2 ≤ n`): `F` divides none of
    `X 0`, `X 1`, `X 2`.
- `exists_isTransversePosition` (`1 ≤ n`): there is an invertible `M` with
  `IsTransversePosition (linearSubst M F)`. T1 (d) gives a line `L` through a
  non-terrible point off `C`, meeting `C` transversally and avoiding the
  finitely many multiple points (L3/Q14). `L` contains infinitely many
  points, of which finitely many are on `C`. Each tangent at a multiple point
  meets `L` in one point, since `L` passes through no multiple point. So some
  `v ∈ L` is off `C` and off those tangents. Choose `M` sending `L` to `Z`
  and `v` to `[1 : 0 : 0]` (P8/P9 for frames), and transport with
  `mult_linearSubst`, `intersectionNumber_linearSubst` and the tangent
  lemmas under `linearSubst` (Q1, Q3, Q6). This is the "use" of the
  predicate that `AGENTS.md` asks for. The function-field statements are not
  transported along `M`. The milestone states the explicit formula in these
  coordinates, as the atlas does.

### `CanonicalDivisor/Differential.lean`

The elements of `K` are values of forms at the point
`chartCoord hF hi : Fin 3 → K`, that is `(X₀/Xᵢ, X₁/Xᵢ, X₂/Xᵢ)`, for a
coordinate with `hi : ¬ F ∣ X i`. In the chart `i = 2`, with
`h2 : ¬ F ∣ X 2`: `x = chartCoord hF h2 0`, `y = chartCoord hF h2 1`,
`f_x = aeval (chartCoord hF h2) (pderiv 0 F)` and
`f_y = aeval (chartCoord hF h2) (pderiv 1 F)`. They are D4's quotients of
forms by `aeval_chartCoord_eq_formQuotient`, and nonzero when `F` does not
divide the form (`aeval_chartCoord_eq_zero_iff`).

- **`chartCoord hF hi`**, with API:
  - `chartCoord_def` (its body, `functionField.mk` of `X j` over `X i`) and
    `chartCoord_self` (`@[simp]`: the `i`-th entry is `1`);
  - `aeval_chartCoord`: for `hG : G.IsHomogeneous m`, `G` at `chartCoord` is
    the fraction `functionField.mk hG (isHomogeneous_X_pow i m) _`;
  - `aeval_chartCoord_eq_formQuotient`: if moreover `hFG : ¬ F ∣ G`, it is
    `formQuotient hF hG (isHomogeneous_X_pow i m) hFG _`, D4's `G / Xᵢᵐ`.
    This is the lemma "`G(x, y, 1) = G / Zᵐ`" that piece 5 uses; it is in
    this file, not in Q5's;
  - `aeval_chartCoord_self` (`@[simp]`: `F` vanishes at `chartCoord`) and
    `aeval_chartCoord_eq_zero_iff` (`G` vanishes there iff `F ∣ G`);
  - `ord_aeval_chartCoord` (`[IsAlgClosed k]`, `¬ F ∣ G`): the order of `G`
    at `chartCoord` at a place `P` is
    `(formDivisor hF hG).coeff P - m * (formDivisor hF (isHomogeneous_X k i)).coeff P`,
    from `principal_formQuotient`.
- `fx_smul_D_add_fy_smul_D (h2 : ¬ F ∣ X 2)`:
  `f_x • D k K x + f_y • D k K y = 0`. Apply the chain rule
  `Derivation.map_aeval_mvPolynomial` to `F` itself at `chartCoord hF h2`:
  `F` vanishes there, and the third coordinate is `1`, whose `D` is 0. No
  dehomogenization is used.
- **`chartDifferential hF (h2 : ¬ F ∣ X 2) : Ω[K⁄k]`**: `f_y⁻¹ • D k K x`,
  Fulton's `dx/f_y`, with `chartDifferential_def`. It uses the field
  inverse, so it takes no other hypothesis. API, each lemma taking only the
  divisibility hypotheses it uses:
  - `chartDifferential_eq (h2) (h0 : ¬ F ∣ pderiv 0 F) (h1 : ¬ F ∣ pderiv 1 F)`:
    `= -(f_x⁻¹ • D k K y)`, from `fx_smul_D_add_fy_smul_D`;
  - `chartDifferential_ne_zero [PerfectField k] (h2) (h1 : ¬ F ∣ pderiv 1 F)`.
    If `D x = 0`, then `f_y • D y = 0`, so `D y = 0`. Then `D` vanishes on
    `K`, since every element of `K` is `f(x, y, 1) / g(x, y, 1)`
    (`functionField.mk_eq_aeval_div_aeval`) and by the chain rule. That
    contradicts Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.D_ne_zero_of_separating` for a separating
    element from
    `TauCeti.IsFunctionField.exists_transcendental_and_isSeparable_adjoin_of_perfectField`.
- Order lemmas, one for each kind of place, with `[IsAlgClosed k]`,
  `hpos : IsTransversePosition F` and `hn : 2 ≤ n`, about
  `ω₀ = chartDifferential hF (hpos.not_dvd_X hF hn 2)`. Write `div ω` for
  `kaehlerDifferentialDivisor ω _`, and `E_{n−3}^Z` for
  `((n : ℤ) - 3) • formDivisor hF (isHomogeneous_X k 2) - adjointDivisor hF`:
  - `coeff_kaehlerDifferentialDivisor_chartDifferential_le_of_isOrdinaryMultiplePoint`:
    at a place `P` with `hP2 : eval (center hF P).1.rep (X 2) ≠ 0` and
    `hP : IsOrdinaryMultiplePoint F (center hF P).1`, over an affine
    ordinary multiple point. It takes this hypothesis about the one point
    instead of the global `hord`; piece 5 derives it from `hord`;
  - `coeff_kaehlerDifferentialDivisor_chartDifferential_le_of_isSimplePoint`:
    at a place with `hP2` as above and `hP : IsSimplePoint F (center hF P).1`;
  - `coeff_kaehlerDifferentialDivisor_chartDifferential_le_of_eval_X_two_eq_zero`:
    at a place with `eval (center hF P).1.rep (X 2) = 0`, over a point of `Z`.

  Each states `(div ω₀).coeff P ≤ (E_{n−3}^Z).coeff P`, by the computations
  in [Approach](#approach). The first two go through private lemmas that
  give `(div ω₀).coeff P` as `-ord_P(f_x)` or `-ord_P(f_y)` from a
  uniformizer `t` with `D t = D y` or `D t = D x`. They use:
  - D1's `div (f • ω) = div f + div ω` and its uniformizer lemma (`D t` has
    order 0 where `t` has order 1);
  - D4's `principal_formQuotient` and `coeff_formDivisor`, through
    `ord_aeval_chartCoord`;
  - V3's `ordForm_pos_iff` (`ord_P(Z) = 0` at affine places) and its unique
    place over a simple point;
  - V8 through D4's `sum_coeff_formDivisor_center` (`I(Q, C ∩ G) = Σ ord`);
  - B3 (1) and its "a non-tangent line through the point has order 1";
  - D5's `coeff_adjointDivisor`.

  Each lemma is 30–80 lines. Split them further into private steps if they
  grow.
- `kaehlerDifferentialDivisor_chartDifferential_le`: `div ω₀ ≤ E_{n−3}^Z`,
  from the three lemmas, since every place has its centre on `Z` or off it
  (V2's `center`).
- `kaehlerDifferentialDivisor_chartDifferential`: `div ω₀ = E_{n−3}^Z`.
  Apply `TauCeti.Divisor.degree_eq_zero_iff` to `E_{n−3}^Z − div ω₀ ≥ 0`. Its
  degree is 0 by D9's `deg E_{n−3} = 2g − 2` (at `L = Z`) and D1's degree
  `2g − 2` of a Kähler divisor.
- `nonneg_kaehlerDifferentialDivisor_iff` (`n ≥ 3`): for `ω : Ω[K⁄k]`,
  `(∀ hω : ω ≠ 0, 0 ≤ kaehlerDifferentialDivisor ω hω) ↔ ∃ H ∈ adjointSubmodule F (n - 3), ω = aeval (chartCoord hF h2) H • chartDifferential hF h2`,
  with `h2 := hpos.not_dvd_X hF _ 2`. This is the book's
  `H(x, y, 1) dx/f_y`. The coefficient `aeval (chartCoord hF h2) H` is D8's
  map `V_m → L(E_m)` at `L = Z`, `H ↦ H/Z^{n−3}`, coerced to `K`:
  `coe_adjointToRiemannRochSpace_apply` writes that map as
  `functionField.mk` of `H` over `Z^{n−3}`, and `aeval_chartCoord` writes
  `aeval (chartCoord hF h2) H` as the same fraction.
  Any `ω` is `h • ω₀` (`Ω[K⁄k]` has rank one). Then
  `div ω = div h + div ω₀ ≥ 0` iff
  `h ∈ L(E_{n−3}^Z)`, and D8's surjectivity gives `H`. If D1 or D13 defines a
  Kähler `Ω(D)` submodule, state this as an equality of submodules with it
  instead.

## The milestone

In `CanonicalDivisor/Differential.lean`, one theorem, proposed name
`canonical_eq_formDivisor_sub_adjointDivisor`, whose docstring ends with
"Milestone: *Canonical divisors of a plane curve are cut out by adjoints of
degree n − 3*." It is the conjunction of the lemmas above, for `F` as in
[Definitions and API](#definitions-and-api):

1. for every nonzero form `G` of degree `n − 3` (`n ≥ 3`), `div G − E` is
   canonical. For `n = 3`, `−E` is canonical. More generally,
   `(n − 3) div L − E` is canonical for every linear `L` with `F ∤ L` and
   every `n ≥ 1` (the atlas's general form);
2. if `IsTransversePosition F` and `2 ≤ n`, then
   `kaehlerDifferentialDivisor (chartDifferential hF h2) _ = (n − 3) div(Z) − E`;
3. every canonical `W` has `deg W = 2g − 2` and `g ≤ ℓ(W)` (in fact `= g`);
4. for `n ≥ 3`: the effective canonical divisors are exactly the
   `div G − E` with `0 ≠ G ∈ V_{n−3}`; `finrank V_{n−3} = g`; and, in the
   coordinates of 2, the holomorphic differentials are exactly the
   `aeval (chartCoord hF h2) H • chartDifferential hF h2`
   (`H(x, y, 1) dx/f_y`) with `H ∈ V_{n−3}`.

The statement is
the atlas's, in its generality or more: any characteristic, `n ≥ 1` for the
general form, and `ℓ(W) = g` instead of `≥`.
