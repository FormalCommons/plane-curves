# Design of part T4: reduction to ordinary multiple points

Part T4 of [`DESIGN.md`](../DESIGN.md#stage-3-555-56-and-72-74) proves the
milestone **7-reduce**, *Reduction of a plane curve to ordinary multiple points
by quadratic transformations*, of
[`atlas/03-resolution-of-singularities.md`](https://github.com/FormalCommons/plane-curves-atlas/blob/main/atlas/03-resolution-of-singularities.md#74-quadratic-transformations)
(Fulton §7.4, Theorem 2, Problems 7.13 and 7.14 (b); §7.5, Problem 7.21 (a),

become one issue and one pull request of a few hundred lines.

The library declarations named here were located in the repository and in
the pinned sources (Tau Ceti `cd41bff`, Mathlib `048fffa`); nothing was

## Notation

Everything is in the namespace `PlaneCurves.Projective`, over a field `k`
with `[IsAlgClosed k]` unless stated. `F : MvPolynomial (Fin 3) k` is an
irreducible form of degree `n` (`F.IsHomogeneous n`, `Irreducible F`). The
notation of [`design/T3.md`](T3.md#notation) is used throughout:
`fundamentalPoint i`, `fundamentalTriangle`, `quadraticMap`,
`properTransform`, `IsExcellentPosition`, `IsQuadraticTransformAt`,
`pointsOverCentre`, `virtualGenus` (`g*`), all already in the library
(T2, T3). `T := map M.mulVecLin hM` is the projective change of coordinates of
an invertible matrix `M` (`hM : Function.Injective M.mulVecLin`, as in
`IsQuadraticTransformAt`), and `F^T = linearSubst M F`.

- `N(F)` is the number of non-ordinary multiple points of `F`, and
  `μ(F) := N(F) + g*(F)`, a natural number by `virtualGenus_nonneg`.
- "Only ordinary multiple points" is written inline, as part B5 does:
  `∀ Q, IsMultiplePoint G Q → IsOrdinaryMultiplePoint G Q`. No predicate is
  added for it.

## The mathematics, and the route

**One step at a non-ordinary centre.** Let `F` be in excellent position with
centre `P = fundamentalPoint 2` a non-ordinary multiple point, and `F'` its
proper transform. By T3's `isMultiplePoint_properTransform_iff` the multiple
points of `F'` are

1. off the triangle, `quadraticMap Q` for the multiple points `Q ≠ P` of `F`
   (all multiple points of `F` other than `P` are off the triangle, by
   `IsExcellentPosition.notMem_fundamentalTriangle_of_isMultiplePoint`), with
   the same multiplicity and ordinariness
   (`mult_properTransform_of_notMem_fundamentalTriangle`,
   `isOrdinaryMultiplePoint_properTransform_iff_of_notMem_fundamentalTriangle`,
   and `bijOn_quadraticMap_compl_fundamentalTriangle`);
2. fundamental points, all ordinary
   (`isOrdinaryMultiplePoint_properTransform_fundamentalPoint`);
3. points over the centre, finitely many (`pointsOverCentre_finite`).

So the non-ordinary multiple points of `F'` are the images of those of `F`
other than `P`, together with the non-ordinary ones over the centre:

```
N(F') = N(F) − 1 + #{Q ∈ pointsOverCentre F | Q non-ordinary multiple on F'}
g*(F') = g*(F) − Σ_{Q ∈ pointsOverCentre F} C(m_Q(F'), 2)        (T3, Step (8))
```

and every non-ordinary multiple point `Q` over the centre has
`C(m_Q(F'), 2) ≥ 1`. Hence `μ(F') ≤ μ(F) − 1`. This is the atlas's proof
route; it is pure counting over T3's results.

**One step at a simple centre or a centre off the curve** (Problem 7.14). If
`m_P(F) ≤ 1` or `P` is ordinary, the points over the centre are simple
(`isSimplePoint_of_mem_pointsOverCentre`), so `N(F') = N(F)`, and
`g*(F') = g*(F)` (`IsQuadraticTransformAt.virtualGenus_eq`). The degree
becomes `2n − m_P(F)`.

**Characteristic `p` (Appendix A).** T3 gives a quadratic transformation
centred at every point that is not terrible (`exists_isQuadraticTransformAt`).
Let `P` be a terrible non-ordinary multiple point, `r = m_P(F)`, so
`(n − r : k) = 0` (`IsTerriblePoint.natCast_sub_eq_zero`) and `n ≥ 2`
(`IsTerriblePoint.two_le_totalDegree`). Choose `m ∈ {0, 1}` with
`(n − m : k) ≠ 0`: `m = 0` if `(n : k) ≠ 0`, else `m = 1`, since then
`(n − 1 : k) = −1`. Choose a point `Q ≠ P` with `m_Q(F) = m`: off the curve
(`exists_eval_rep_ne_zero_notMem`) or a simple point (the curve is infinite,
`zeroLocus_singleton_infinite`, and has finitely many multiple points,
`finite_setOf_isMultiplePoint_of_irreducible`). `Q` is not terrible, since
`P` is and there is at most one terrible point
(`IsTerriblePoint.mk_eq_mk`; `k` is perfect, being algebraically closed).

Apply a quadratic transformation centred at `Q` **whose fundamental triangle
avoids `P`**. T3's `exists_isExcellentPosition` takes no set to avoid; piece
T4.1 adds one. Its proof is T3's proof with two more exclusions, both
finite: the two lines through `Q` are chosen among the cofinitely many good
lines of `eventually_cofinite_ncard_zeroLocus_pair_diff_eq_of_not_isTerriblePoint`
and also off the finitely many lines joining `Q` to the points of `S`
(`finite_setOf_eval_sum_C_mul_X_eq_zero_of_finite`, used the same way in
`exists_ncard_zeroLocus_pair_sum_C_mul_X_eq`), and the third line is chosen by
`exists_ncard_zeroLocus_pair_sum_C_mul_X_eq` with `S` added to the finite set
it avoids (`S` contains no point equal to `Q`).

Write `P = T R` with `R ∉ fundamentalTriangle`. The new curve `G` has degree
`n' = 2n − m` (`IsQuadraticTransformAt.isHomogeneous`), and `P* := quadraticMap R`
is a multiple point of `G` of multiplicity `r`, non-ordinary like `P`
(`mult_linearSubst`, `isOrdinaryMultiplePoint_linearSubst_iff`, and T3's
transfer off the triangle). `P*` is not terrible for `G`: otherwise
`(n' − r : k) = 0` by `IsTerriblePoint.natCast_sub_eq_zero`; but
`n' − r = (n − m) + (n − r)` in `ℕ` (as `m ≤ 1 < n`, `r < n` by
`IsTerriblePoint.mult_lt`), so `(n' − r : k) = (n − m : k) ≠ 0`. The auxiliary
step leaves `N` and `g*` unchanged (previous paragraph), and the step centred
at `P*` in `G` lowers `μ` by at least one. So in every characteristic `μ`
drops by at least one in at most two steps, and in characteristic 0 (no
terrible points, `not_isTerriblePoint`) in one step. Induction on `μ(F)`
gives the theorem, with at most `2 μ(F)` steps in general and at most
`μ(F) = N + g*(F)` steps in characteristic 0, Fulton's bound.

**The strong forms.** The same avoidance does all three.

- (a) (Problem 7.13). Let `S` be a finite set of simple points of `F`. At each
  step the centre is a multiple point, or an auxiliary point chosen outside
  `S`, so it is not in `S`, and T4.1 puts the triangle off `S`. Off the
  triangle, the step is a bijection of the curves (`bijOn_quadraticMap`)
  preserving multiplicities and local rings (piece T4.3). So at the end, on
  the complement in `C` of a finite set `B` disjoint from `S` (the union of
  the pull-backs of the triangles, met by `C` in finitely many points), the
  composite of the point maps is injective, onto a cofinite subset of the
  last curve, and induces isomorphisms of local rings, all compatible with
  one isomorphism of function fields. Taking `B` to contain the multiple
  points of `F` makes the image consist of simple points. Every open
  neighbourhood of `S` in a curve is cofinite, so this is the atlas's (a), in
  the library's representation (points of `ℙ²`, `localSubring`,
  `functionField`).
- (b) (Problem 7.14 (b)). After reaching ordinary multiple points, apply steps
  centred at non-terrible points off the curve and outside `S`: they double
  the degree and keep `N = 0` and `g*` (one-step lemma above), and they keep
  `S` simple, as in (a).
- (c) (Problem 7.21 (a), (c)). In the library a nonsingular projective curve
  `X` is the set of places of its function field (the nonsingular model, V5),
  and the birational morphism `X → C` is the centre map `center` composed
  with an isomorphism of function fields. Given an irreducible `F` and a
  finite set `𝒫` of places of `k(F)`, first apply (a) with `S` the simple
  centres of places of `𝒫` to reach ordinary multiple points. Then, while
  some place `p ∈ 𝒫` is centred at a multiple point `P`, now ordinary, apply
  a step centred at `P` (preceded by the auxiliary step if `P` is terrible),
  with triangle avoiding the simple centres of `𝒫`. The new centre of `p` is
  a point over the centre, hence simple: in the coordinates of the step,
  `X` and `Y` are lines through the ordinary point `P` that are not tangent
  (good position), so `ord_p(X) = ord_p(Y) = 1`
  (B3's `ordForm_eq_one_of_not_isTangentAt`), and the quadratic
  transformation of function fields sends `X/Y` to `Y/X`, of order 0, and
  `Z/Y` to `Y/Z`, of positive order. So the centre of the moved place is a
  point `[α : 1 : 0]` with `α ≠ 0`, a point over the centre, simple by
  `isSimplePoint_of_mem_pointsOverCentre`. Places centred at the other
  multiple points move with their centres (off the triangle), places centred
  at simple points stay simple, and `N` stays 0. The number of places of `𝒫`
  centred at multiple points drops, so this terminates; then (b) raises the
  degree. Nothing here uses characteristic 0.

**Characteristic.** The atlas states the strong forms in characteristic 0
and says that the book does not address them in characteristic `p` (Notes on
the source). The route above proves them in every characteristic: the only
characteristic-dependent input is Lemma 1, which T3 already proves at every
non-terrible point, and every centre used here is non-terrible. This is more
general than the atlas's statement, so it needs no `ERRATA.md` entry; the
atlas only leaves the question open.

## Definitions and their API

### Non-ordinary multiple points (`Singularities/NonOrdinary.lean`)

- `nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints (F : MvPolynomial (Fin 3) k) : Set (ℙ k (Fin 3 → k))`:
  `{P | IsMultiplePoint F P ∧ ¬ IsOrdinaryMultiplePoint F P}`. `N(F)` is its
  `Set.ncard`. API:
  - `mem_nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints_iff`;
  - `nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints_finite` (`Irreducible F`, `[PerfectField k]`),
    from `finite_setOf_isMultiplePoint_of_irreducible`;
  - `nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints_smul` (`c ≠ 0`);
  - `nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints_linearSubst`:
    `nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints (linearSubst M F) = map M.mulVecLin hM ⁻¹' nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints F`
    (`mult_linearSubst`, `isOrdinaryMultiplePoint_linearSubst_iff`), and
    `ncard_nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints_linearSubst` (`Set.ncard_preimage_of_injective_subset_range`
    or `Set.ncard_image_of_injective`, `T` being bijective,
    `bijective_map_mulVecLin`);
  - `nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints_eq_empty_iff`: `= ∅ ↔ ∀ Q, IsMultiplePoint F Q → IsOrdinaryMultiplePoint F Q`;
  - examples: a nonsingular curve and a nodal cubic have none; a cuspidal
    cubic has one (T3's examples and `virtualGenus_eq_zero_of_isMultiplePoint`
    give the curves).

### The step relation (`Resolution.lean`)

- `IsQuadraticTransform (F G : MvPolynomial (Fin 3) k) : Prop := ∃ P, IsQuadraticTransformAt F P G`:
  "`G` is a quadratic transformation of `F`". A sequence `F = F₁, …, F_m` is
  `List.IsChain IsQuadraticTransform (F :: l)` with `F_m = (F :: l).getLast _`,
  and `l.length = m − 1` counts the steps. Mathlib has no counted power of a
  relation; `List.IsChain` with `List.length` is the counted form, and
  `List.relationReflTransGen_of_exists_isChain_cons` /
  `List.exists_isChain_cons_of_relationReflTransGen`
  (`Mathlib/Data/List/Chain.lean`) translate to and from
  `Relation.ReflTransGen IsQuadraticTransform`, which the API lemmas below are
  stated for. API:
  - `isQuadraticTransform_def`;
  - `IsQuadraticTransform.exists_isHomogeneous` (`∃ d, G.IsHomogeneous d`,
    from `IsQuadraticTransformAt.isHomogeneous`), `IsQuadraticTransform.irreducible`;
  - `IsQuadraticTransform.virtualGenus_le` (`g*(G) ≤ g*(F)`, from
    `IsQuadraticTransformAt.virtualGenus` and `finsum_nonneg`);
  - `IsQuadraticTransform.nonempty_algEquiv` (T4.3): for `hF`, `hG` and
    `Fact (Irreducible _)` instances,
    `Nonempty (functionField hF ≃ₐ[k] functionField hG)`;
  - the same three for `Relation.ReflTransGen IsQuadraticTransform F G`,
    named `irreducible_of_reflTransGen_isQuadraticTransform`,
    `virtualGenus_le_of_reflTransGen_isQuadraticTransform`,
    `nonempty_algEquiv_of_reflTransGen_isQuadraticTransform`, by
    `Relation.ReflTransGen.head_induction_on`. These are what D10 uses.

No other definition is needed. The measure `μ` is written out
(`(nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints F).ncard + (virtualGenus F).toNat`), and the
avoidance data of (a) are explicit hypotheses and conclusions.

## Main results

### Excellent position avoiding a finite set (`Position.lean`, T4.1)

- `exists_isExcellentPosition_avoiding (hF) (hirr) (hP : ¬ IsTerriblePoint F P.rep) (hS : S.Finite) (hPS : P ∉ S)`:
  `∃ M hM, map M.mulVecLin hM (fundamentalPoint 2) = P ∧ IsExcellentPosition (linearSubst M F) ∧ ∀ R, map M.mulVecLin hM R ∈ S → R ∉ fundamentalTriangle`.
  The last conjunct says that `T⁻¹(S)` misses `V(XYZ)` (Problem 7.13 (a)),
  stated without `T⁻¹`. T3's `exists_isExcellentPosition` becomes its
  corollary with `S = ∅` (its proof is replaced, its statement and uses are
  unchanged).
- `exists_isQuadraticTransformAt_avoiding`: the same, with
  `IsQuadraticTransformAt F P (properTransform (linearSubst M F))`.

### One step (`Singularities/NonOrdinary.lean`, T4.2)

For `h : IsExcellentPosition F`, `F' = properTransform F`, `P₀ = fundamentalPoint 2`:

- `IsExcellentPosition.nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints_properTransform`:
  `nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints F' = quadraticMap '' (nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints F \ {P₀}) ∪ (nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints F' ∩ pointsOverCentre F)`.
  From `isMultiplePoint_properTransform_iff`, the transfer lemmas off the
  triangle, the ordinariness of the fundamental points, and
  `notMem_fundamentalTriangle_of_isMultiplePoint` (to identify
  `nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints F ∩ fundamentalTriangleᶜ` with
  `nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints F \ {P₀}`).
- `IsExcellentPosition.ncard_nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints_properTransform_le`:
  `N(F') + 1 ≤ N(F) + #(nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints F' ∩ pointsOverCentre F)`
  when `P₀ ∈ nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints F` (`Set.ncard_union_le`,
  `Set.InjOn.ncard_image` with `quadraticMap_quadraticMap`,
  `Set.ncard_sdiff_singleton_add_one`).
- `IsExcellentPosition.ncard_nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints_properTransform`:
  `N(F') = N(F)` when `mult F P₀ ≤ 1 ∨ IsOrdinaryMultiplePoint F P₀`.
- `IsExcellentPosition.ncard_add_virtualGenus_properTransform_lt`
  (`Irreducible F`): `(N(F') : ℤ) + g*(F') < N(F) + g*(F)` when
  `P₀ ∈ nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints F`. With `virtualGenus_properTransform`:
  each `Q` in `nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints F' ∩ pointsOverCentre F` has
  `1 ≤ C(m_Q(F'), 2)`, and the other terms are `≥ 0`
  (`finsum_mem_le_finsum_mem`/`Set.ncard_eq_toFinset_card'` and
  `Finset.card_le_sum_of_one_le`, or a direct `finsum` bound; the piece picks
  the shortest).
- Through `linearSubst`: `IsQuadraticTransformAt.ncard_add_virtualGenus_lt`
  (`P ∈ nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints F → μ(G) < μ(F)`, in `ℤ`) and
  `IsQuadraticTransformAt.ncard_nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints_eq`
  (`mult F P ≤ 1 ∨ IsOrdinaryMultiplePoint F P → N(G) = N(F)`), with
  `ncard_nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints_linearSubst` and `virtualGenus_linearSubst`.

### Local rings and function fields along a step (`Birational.lean`, `Resolution.lean`; T4.3)

- In `Birational.lean`, for `[Fact (Irreducible F)]`, `[Fact (Irreducible (properTransform F))]`,
  no `X i ∣ F`, and `R ∈ zeroLocus {F} \ fundamentalTriangle`:
  `map_localSubring_quadraticFunctionFieldEquiv`:
  `(localSubring hF ⟨R, _⟩).map (quadraticFunctionFieldEquiv hF hX) = localSubring hF.properTransform ⟨quadraticMap R, _⟩`.
  Proof: `G/H ↦ G^Q/H^Q` (`quadraticFunctionFieldEquiv_mk`) and
  `H^Q(v) = H(Qv)` (`eval_quadraticTransform`), so `H(R) ≠ 0` gives
  `H^Q(quadraticMap R) ≠ 0` up to the nonzero factor `(xyz)^d`; this is `≤`,
  and `≥` is the same for the inverse (`quadraticFunctionFieldEquiv_symm_mk`,
  `quadraticMap_quadraticMap`). The membership criterion is
  `mem_localSubring_iff` (`Projective/CurveLocalRing.lean`). Compare
  `map_localSubring_linearSubstEquiv` (`Place/ChangeOfCoordinates.lean`), the
  same statement for a linear change of coordinates.
- In `Resolution.lean`: `IsQuadraticTransformAt.exists_algEquiv`: there are
  `M`, `hM` as in `IsQuadraticTransformAt` and
  `e : functionField hF ≃ₐ[k] functionField hG` (the composite of
  `functionField.linearSubstEquiv` of `Place/ChangeOfCoordinates.lean`,
  `quadraticFunctionFieldEquiv`, and `ProjectiveSpace.functionField.equivOfEq`
  for `G = properTransform (linearSubst M F)`) with
  `(localSubring hF ⟨T R, _⟩).map e = localSubring hG ⟨quadraticMap R, _⟩`
  for `R ∈ zeroLocus {linearSubst M F} \ fundamentalTriangle`. With the
  avoidance of T4.1 it is the step lemma of (a).
  `IsQuadraticTransform.nonempty_algEquiv` is its corollary.

### The reduction (`Resolution.lean`, T4.4)

- `exists_isQuadraticTransformAt_of_isTerriblePoint` (the auxiliary step):
  for `P` terrible and a finite `S` of simple points with `P ∉ S`, there are
  `Q ∉ S ∪ {P}` with `mult F Q ≤ 1`, `M`, `hM` and `G` with
  `IsQuadraticTransformAt F Q G` through `M` (the four conjuncts of the
  definition), `T⁻¹(S ∪ {P})` off the triangle, and
  `¬ IsTerriblePoint G (quadraticMap R).rep` where `T R = P`. Proof as in
  [the route](#the-mathematics-and-the-route).
- `exists_reduction_avoiding` (the core): for a finite set `S` of simple
  points of `F`, there are `l`, a final curve `G = (F :: l).getLast _`, a finite
  set `B` with `Disjoint S B` and containing the multiple points of `F`, a map
  `φ : ℙ² → ℙ²`, and `e : functionField hF ≃ₐ[k] functionField hG` (in the
  statement, with the `Fact` instances introduced by `haveI`) such that
  1. `List.IsChain IsQuadraticTransform (F :: l)`, `G` irreducible of some
     degree, with only ordinary multiple points;
  2. `l.length ≤ 2 * μ(F)`, and `l.length ≤ μ(F)` if `CharZero k`;
  3. `g*(G) ≤ g*(F)`;
  4. `Set.InjOn φ (zeroLocus {F} \ B)`, `φ '' (zeroLocus {F} \ B)` is
     cofinite in `zeroLocus {G}`, every point of it is simple on `G`, and
     `(localSubring hF ⟨R, _⟩).map e = localSubring hG ⟨φ R, _⟩` for
     `R ∈ zeroLocus {F} \ B`.

  By strong induction on `μ(F)` (`Nat.strong_induction_on`), carrying `S`
  (replaced by its image at each step), `B` (enlarged by the pull-back of
  each triangle, finite on `C` by `finite_zeroLocus_pair_of_isRelPrime`; no
  line is a component of an irreducible `F` of degree `≥ 2`, and a line or a
  conic has `N = 0` already), `φ` (composed) and `e` (composed, with
  `Subring.map_map`). The base case, `N(F) = 0`, is `l = []`, `φ = id`,
  `e = AlgEquiv.refl`. If the piece finds the four conjuncts too heavy for
  one induction, it states 1–3 first (`exists_reduction`) and adds 4 in T4.5
  by the same induction; the API above does not change.

### Strong forms (`Resolution.lean`, T4.5)

- `exists_reduction_avoiding_of_le_degree` ((a) and (b)): the core with,
  moreover, `d ≤ deg G` for any given `d`, by appending steps centred at
  non-terrible points off the curve and outside `S` (degree `2n`,
  `IsQuadraticTransformAt.virtualGenus_eq`,
  `IsQuadraticTransformAt.ncard_nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints_eq`); no step bound.
- `exists_ordinary_model` (for D10): there is an irreducible form `G` with
  only ordinary multiple points, `g*(G) ≤ g*(F)` and
  `Nonempty (functionField hF ≃ₐ[k] functionField hG)`.

### Places (`Place/QuadraticTransformation.lean`, T4.6)

For `h : IsExcellentPosition F` with `F` irreducible, `e := quadraticFunctionFieldEquiv hF h.not_X_dvd`,
and a place `p` of `k(F)`:

- `center_map_quadraticFunctionFieldEquiv` (centre off the triangle): if
  `center hF p ∉ fundamentalTriangle` then
  `(center hF.properTransform (p.map e)).1 = quadraticMap (center hF p).1`,
  from T4.3 and `center_eq_iff_le`.
- `ordForm_map_quadraticFunctionFieldEquiv_sub`: for forms `G₁`, `G₂` of the
  same degree, the difference of the orders of `G₁`, `G₂` at `p.map e` is the
  difference of the orders of `G₁^Q`, `G₂^Q` at `p` (by `ordForm_def` /
  `ordForm_eq_addValuation_mk` and `quadraticFunctionFieldEquiv_symm_mk`).
  Only differences are invariant, since `ordForm` is normalised by a form
  not vanishing at the centre.
- `center_map_quadraticFunctionFieldEquiv_mem_pointsOverCentre`: if
  `center hF p = fundamentalPoint 2` and that point is ordinary, then the
  centre of `p.map e` is in `pointsOverCentre F`, hence simple
  (`isSimplePoint_of_mem_pointsOverCentre`). Proof: by
  `ordForm_eq_one_of_not_isTangentAt` (B3; `X`, `Y` are not tangent by good
  position), `ord_p(X) = ord_p(Y) = 1` and `ord_p(Z) = 0`; with the previous
  lemma, `X/Y` has order 0 and `Z/Y` positive order at `p.map e`, so the
  centre is `[α : 1 : 0]` with `α ≠ 0` (`ordForm_pos_iff`,
  `ordForm_eq_zero_iff`).
- Through `linearSubst`, with `center_map_linearSubstEquiv_eq_iff` and

### Plane models and the milestone (`Resolution.lean`, T4.7)

- `exists_reduction_places` ((c)): for an irreducible form `F`, a finite set
  `𝒫` of places of `k(F)` and `d : ℕ`, there are an irreducible form `G` of
  degree at least `d` with only ordinary multiple points, a chain
  `List.IsChain IsQuadraticTransform (F :: l)` ending at `G`, and
  `e : functionField hF ≃ₐ[k] functionField hG` with
  `IsSimplePoint G (center hG (p.map e)).1` for every `p ∈ 𝒫`. Induction on
  the number of places of `𝒫` centred at multiple points, as in
  [the route](#the-mathematics-and-the-route), after
  `exists_reduction_avoiding` with `S` the simple centres of `𝒫`.
- `exists_planeModel_ordinary` ((c) in Fulton's words): for a function field
  `K` of one variable over `k` and finitely many places of `K`, there is an
  irreducible plane curve `G` of arbitrarily large degree with only ordinary
  multiple points and `K ≃ₐ[k] k(G)` under which every given place is
  centred at a simple point. From `exists_reduction_places` and a plane model
  of `K`: `TauCeti.IsFunctionField.exists_ker_aeval_eq_span_singleton_of_perfectField`
  (`FieldTheory/FunctionField/PlaneModel.lean`, affine) with
  `functionField.projectiveClosureEquiv` (`ProjectiveSpace/ChartLocalRing.lean`).
  The morphism `X → C` of the atlas is the centre map on the places of `K`,
  which are the nonsingular model (V5, departure 7 of `DESIGN.md`).
- **The milestone** `reduction_ordinaryMultiplePoints`, one theorem, the
  conjunction of
  1. there is `l` with `List.IsChain IsQuadraticTransform (F :: l)` whose last
     term has only ordinary multiple points (any characteristic);
  2. if `CharZero k`, such an `l` with
     `(l.length : ℤ) ≤ (nonOrdinaryMultiplePoints F).ncard + virtualGenus F`;
  3. (a) and (b): `exists_reduction_avoiding_of_le_degree`;
  4. (c): `exists_reduction_places` and `exists_planeModel_ordinary`.

  Its docstring ends with "Milestone: *Reduction of a plane curve to ordinary
  multiple points by quadratic transformations*." It is in the atlas's
  generality or more: (1) as stated; (2) is Fulton's bound; (a)–(c) hold in
  every characteristic, not only in characteristic 0. (a) is stated for
  cofinite subsets of the curve, which are its open subsets, and with local
  rings compatible with one isomorphism of function fields, the library's
  representation of an isomorphism of open subsets of curves.

## Notes

- **No errata.** The atlas already records the problems of the source that
  T4 meets: Appendix A's auxiliary centre must itself be non-terrible, and
  "`p ∤ n − r`" should read `n' − r` (Notes on the source); T4 follows both.
  The strong forms in characteristic `p` are an open question in the atlas,
  not an error; T4 answers it (they hold), which is more general than the
  milestone's statement.
- **What D10 needs** (8-virtual, `g ≤ g*`): `exists_ordinary_model`, with the
  genus formula (D9) on the ordinary model and birational invariance of the
  genus (`TauCeti.genus_eq_of_ringEquiv`). D10 needs nothing about the steps
  themselves.
- **What T5 needs** (Noether's formula, global form): the sequence and the
  points over the centre at each step, which `List.IsChain IsQuadraticTransform`
  and `pointsOverCentre` give, and the one-step formulas of T3. T5 applies
  the reduction to `FG`, a reducible curve without multiple components
  (Problem 7.16 (a)); T3 and T4 are stated for irreducible curves, since
  `exists_isExcellentPosition` and `virtualGenus` (finiteness of multiple
  points) need it. T5's own design must either extend T3's existence of
  excellent position and T4's reduction to squarefree forms (the atlas says
  the argument extends), or derive the global form from B4's local form and
  Bézout, as the atlas recommends; T4 does not decide this.
- **Why not `ReflTransGen` alone.** The milestone counts steps in
  characteristic 0, so the statement uses `List.IsChain` with `List.length`;
  `ReflTransGen` is used for the API lemmas, where the count does not matter.
